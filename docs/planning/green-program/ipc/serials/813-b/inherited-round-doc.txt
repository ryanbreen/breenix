These are source observations; no saturation failure was reproduced here.

Unix legacy termination closes endpoints at
`kernel/src/process/process.rs:597` and
`kernel/src/process/process.rs:679`. `Process::terminate` invokes that cleanup
at `kernel/src/process/process.rs:474`. The four direct termination call sites
are `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:1400`,
`kernel/src/process/manager.rs:1947`,
`kernel/src/signal/delivery.rs:239`, and
`kernel/src/signal/delivery.rs:274`. The adjacent comment at
`kernel/src/signal/delivery.rs:242` is not a fifth call.

Exec has an extracted-descriptor owner at `kernel/src/ipc/fd.rs:755`, delivered
by its Drop at `kernel/src/ipc/fd.rs:759`. Normal exit has extracted cleanup
with its Unix close at `kernel/src/task/process_task.rs:733`. Those mechanisms
do not establish a PM-free notification boundary for the four legacy
termination callers. FD-table Drop also closes Unix directly at
`kernel/src/ipc/fd.rs:701`.

Consequently, adding immediate writer wakes to the legacy Unix close paths would extend
scheduler acquisition under PM; copying the inherited deferred route would
extend the saturation limitation that the supplied plan explicitly rejects.

question was sent: may B inherit the disclosed 936 limitation, or must the
PM-free boundary land first? No answer had arrived when this report was prepared.
