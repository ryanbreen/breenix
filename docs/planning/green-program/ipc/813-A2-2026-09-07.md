## RCA and repair

P-11: `kernel/src/syscall/iovec.rs:26` previously called sys_write separately
for each vector. A request of two 2048-byte vectors could publish its first
half before waiting on its second half, even though the aggregate is PIPE_BUF.
The repair snapshots vector metadata once, checks aggregate length overflow,
and snapshots the pipe/FIFO endpoint and descriptor mode under PM at
`kernel/src/syscall/iovec.rs:37` and `kernel/src/syscall/iovec.rs:57`.
For a nonempty aggregate at most 4096, it gathers bytes into one kernel buffer
and calls the shared write_pipe adapter once at
`kernel/src/syscall/iovec.rs:86`. This chooses gathering over a buffer-lock hold
across userspace vectors: user copies finish before the pipe lock, and the
existing adapter retains its request-size predicate across prepared waits.
Other descriptor kinds and aggregates above PIPE_BUF retain per-vector writes.
The descriptor handle and O_NONBLOCK mode remain stable across this operation.

P-9: EOF is intentional. An empty FIFO with no writer returns 0, including
an O_NONBLOCK reader opened before the first writer. The old phantom writer
reference produced EAGAIN incorrectly. The explicit policy comment is at
`kernel/src/ipc/fifo.rs:66`; new_zero_refs remains at
`kernel/src/ipc/fifo.rs:69`. This follows the no-writer rule in
[POSIX read](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/functions/read.html).
The web fetch of that page was denied with HTTP 403; the rule was also supplied
explicitly in the task. No broader POSIX conformance is inferred.

P-15: the queued path captures its thread identity before enabling preemption
at `kernel/src/syscall/blocking_io.rs:31`. It removes the waiter and normalizes
that same thread unconditionally at `kernel/src/syscall/blocking_io.rs:53` and
`kernel/src/syscall/blocking_io.rs:54`. The identity-taking finish helper at
`kernel/src/task/waitqueue.rs:146` removes that tid and uses with_thread_mut on
that tid. Queued publication requires a current thread while preemption is
disabled; the capture asserts that invariant. There is no optional post-loop
identity lookup that can leave the queued entry behind. Existing callers of
finish_wait keep their current-thread wrapper.

P-13: formatting is isolated in `abd13701`, covering kernel/src/ipc/fd.rs,
kernel/src/ipc/poll.rs, and kernel/src/process/manager.rs. The supplied review
record and GitHub review/comment bodies did not identify the three filenames
by P-13 label. These are the three non-dispatch PR-933 files selected from the
current formatter output; the selection was disclosed while requesting the
review's exact roster. The initial whole-kernel formatter check reports 57
files, so whole-kernel rustfmt cleanliness is not claimed. No unrelated
formatting of protected interrupt/per-CPU paths was attempted. Issue 938
tracks the unresolved roster; P-13 is not declared discharged.

No Tier-1 or Tier-2 file was edited. No interrupt, architecture dispatch,
context-switch, or syscall-entry code was edited, and no logging was added.
The ordinary vectored syscall adapter changes at the defect itself; the
prepared-wait helper and waitqueue cleanup API are ordinary thread-context
code. The shared waitqueue wrapper change also applies to existing finish_wait
callers, preserving removal and blocked-in-syscall normalization.

## Runtime oracle and mutations

The existing driver now declares 15 arms per descriptor kind at
`userspace/programs/src/pipe_fifo_blocking_oracle.rs:18`, for 30 verdicts per
boot. Its new two-writer arm starts at
`userspace/programs/src/pipe_fifo_blocking_oracle.rs:450`. Each child sends its
real tid, performs one writev with two 2048-byte vectors, reports its actual
return, and waits for release before exiting. Initially only 2048 bytes fit.
The parent observes both actual writer wait registrations with unchanged
occupancy, drains the filler, receives two 4096-byte returns, and verifies two
uniform records with distinct tags in either order. It rejects interleaving,
lost/duplicated records, trailing bytes, residual waiter state, and unsuccessful
child reaps. The expected tally is 71680 bytes, including filler.

`userspace/programs/src/pipe_fifo_blocking_oracle.rs:175`. The no_writer_eof arm
at `userspace/programs/src/pipe_fifo_blocking_oracle.rs:571` additionally closes
the writer and checks empty-buffer EOF for pipe and FIFO. Its tally is 0;
that value is an observed read return, not a skipped transfer. ARM evidence is
**FIFO data I/O with fixture-created FIFO**, not public mkfifo coverage.

## 919 scope settlement

The original 919 request was delivery of new last-reader-close writer
notifications from fault termination after a safe lock boundary. The issue's
current body already corrects the original two-caller count to four and records
PR 933's writer repair; it had been left open for the distinct legacy reader-EOF
lock concern. No correction to a still-two-caller body is needed at this HEAD.

Direct caller census: `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:1400` is the
restore-failure caller; `kernel/src/process/manager.rs:1947` is the fault-exit
caller; `kernel/src/signal/delivery.rs:239` and
`kernel/src/signal/delivery.rs:274` are Terminate and CoreDump. The context-switch
PM scopes also cover deliver_pending_signals. These are 4 of 4 source call
sites; a comment containing terminate() was excluded from the call census.
Process::terminate calls close_all_fds at `kernel/src/process/process.rs:474`.

PR 933 provides shared delivery for 4 of 4 callers through the two architecture
close_all_fds bodies at `kernel/src/process/process.rs:541` and
`kernel/src/process/process.rs:623`. Their PipeRead/FifoRead arms deliver the
owned notification at `kernel/src/process/process.rs:566`,
`kernel/src/process/process.rs:604`, `kernel/src/process/process.rs:648`, and
`kernel/src/process/process.rs:686`. No caller-specific new writer notification
is discarded. The PipeWrite/FifoWrite bindings discard an empty new-writer
notification: `kernel/src/ipc/pipe.rs:224` returns writers: None after the legacy
reader wake. That is not a lost writer-close wake.

The delivered mechanism is deferred scheduling under caller-owned PM, rather
than moving cleanup after PM release: CloseNotifications::deliver_deferred
routes to wake_up_deferred and isr_unblock_for_io. The usual enqueue is lock-free;
blanket lock-free delivery is not claimed. The full-buffer thread-context
fallback in `kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:6409` acquires the scheduler, and its
interrupt-context overflow may reject a wake. The legacy reader EOF wake at
`kernel/src/ipc/pipe.rs:187` likewise still acquires the scheduler inline.
Issue 936 retains those concerns. Closing 919 settles its original writer-wake
omission, not those remaining lock-order/saturation concerns. Issue 919 was
closed with that census and scope comment; its final state is retained in
`serials/813-a2/issue-919-state.json`.
