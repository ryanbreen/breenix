# #796 — `fcntl(F_SETFD)` returned `EAGAIN` from process-manager lock contention

Branch: `fix/796-fcntl-eagain-census`. Base: `origin/main` at `814bc652`.

Observed signature (production-profile aarch64 gate, 1 boot in 10 on main
`bdb5be90`, recorded in #796):

```
[TTY_ORACLE:FAIL:cloexec_exec:fcntl_setfd_failed:EAGAIN]
```

`tty_oracle`'s `cloexec_exec` arm calls `fcntl(slave, F_SETFD, FD_CLOEXEC)` as its
first step. `FileDescriptorTable::set_fd_flags` (`kernel/src/ipc/fd.rs`) has no
`EAGAIN` return path — its only error is `EBADF`. The `EAGAIN` was produced
*before* the `F_SETFD` dispatch arm was reached, by `sys_fcntl`'s process-lookup
preamble turning a failed `try_manager()` try-lock into `SyscallResult::Err(11)`.

### (c) How the R22-era lock discipline classifies the PROCESS_MANAGER lock

Sources read: `kernel/src/process/mod.rs` (`manager()`, `try_manager()`,
`with_process_manager()` and their doc comments), the `#745` PM-window
re-derivation comment at `kernel/src/syscall/handlers.rs:1876-1918`,
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/context_switch.rs:6955-6971`,
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:2330-2336`,
`kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:339-357`, and the
`SCHED_AFTER_PM_VIOLATIONS` lock-order marker at
`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:4877` / `:4975`.

1. **Two acquisition modes, deliberately distinguished.**
   `manager()` blocks; on aarch64 it masks `DAIF` *before* taking the mutex and
   releases the mutex *before* restoring `DAIF` (`process/mod.rs:62-72`,
   `:106-127`, `:208-227`). `try_manager()` does not block and does not touch the
   interrupt state: its body is 6 lines (`process/mod.rs:315-321`) and contains 0
   `daif`/`cli`/`sti` operations and 1 `try_lock()`.
   claim-lint:ok: `process/mod.rs:315-321`, quoted in full in the repair section.

2. **Lock order is PM before SCHEDULER, and the two must not nest.**
   `set_next_ttbr0_for_thread` uses `try_manager()` explicitly "to prevent an
   AB-BA deadlock between PROCESS_MANAGER and SCHEDULER locks"
   (`aarch64/context_switch.rs:6962-6964`), and a commit that runs while this CPU
   still owns PM is a *gate-failing* violation counted by
   `SCHED_AFTER_PM_VIOLATIONS` and printed as
   `[EXEC_LOCK_ORDER:VIOLATION:PM_HELD]`.

3. **Is the PM lock taken from IRQ / exception context?** Full grep of the
   interrupt and exception paths
   (`kernel/src/interrupts.rs`, `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs`,
   `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/{exception,context_switch,timer_interrupt,irq}.rs`):

   | site | context | mode |
   |---|---|---|
   | `interrupts.rs:726` | x86 page-fault CoW (synchronous exception) | `try_manager()` — non-blocking, falls back to the direct-CR3 path |
   | `interrupts.rs:965` | x86 syscall/interrupt-return signal delivery | `try_manager()` — non-blocking |
   | `interrupts/context_switch.rs:347`, `:792`, `:920`, `:1397`, `:1742` | x86 timer-IRQ dispatch + signal check (**asynchronous IRQ**) | `try_manager()` — non-blocking; on failure it *refuses the dispatch* and re-arms `need_resched` |
   | `aarch64/context_switch.rs:6965`, `:7216` | aarch64 timer-IRQ dispatch (**asynchronous IRQ**) | `try_manager()` — non-blocking, `TtbrResult::PmLockBusy` |
   | `interrupts.rs:1421`, `:1713` | x86 page-fault / GPF **`if from_userspace` kill arms** (synchronous exception from ring 3) | `with_process_manager()` — **blocking** |
   | `aarch64/exception.rs:447` | aarch64 EL0 fault victim resolution (synchronous exception from EL0) | `with_process_manager()` — **blocking** |
   | `aarch64/exception.rs:2335` | aarch64 CoW data abort from EL0 (synchronous exception from EL0) | `manager()` — **blocking** |

   The aarch64 timer/IRQ files (`timer_interrupt.rs`, `gic.rs`) contain no PM
   access at all.

   **Classification.** The grep finds 13 PM accesses in the interrupt and
   exception files and the table lists 13 of 13: 9 of 13 non-blocking
   `try_manager()`, 4 of 13 blocking. The 7 asynchronous-IRQ accesses (5 in x86
   `interrupts/context_switch.rs`, 2 in aarch64 `context_switch.rs`) are all in
   the non-blocking group, and 0 of the 4 blocking accesses sits in asynchronous
   IRQ context: those 4 are **synchronous traps from userspace** — the same
   context class as a syscall. The aarch64 CoW handler states the safety argument
   verbatim at `exception.rs:2331-2335`:
   claim-lint:ok: 13 of 13 sites are printed in the table directly above.

   > "This is safe because CoW faults from EL0 guarantee the current CPU doesn't
   > hold PROCESS_MANAGER — we were in userspace when the fault occurred. Using
   > try_manager() (non-blocking) would fail under SMP contention when another CPU
   > holds the lock during fork/exec, killing the faulting process."
   >
   > claim-lint:ok: verbatim quote of the comment at
   > `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:2331-2335`; the word "guarantee"
   > is that comment's, reproduced rather than asserted afresh here.

   That is exactly the shape of #796, one trap type over.

   Therefore the STOP condition for this task — *the PM lock is taken **blocking**
   from asynchronous IRQ context, i.e. the #789/#791 class* — is **not** met, and
   repair option (a) is available. The 7 non-blocking IRQ-context accesses are
   disclosed here rather than elided: they exist, they are the reason
   `try_manager()` exists, and 7 of 7 have a degrade path (refuse the dispatch
   and re-arm `need_resched`, or skip the signal check) instead of waiting, so 0
   of the 7 can deadlock against a thread that holds the lock blocking.
   claim-lint:ok: the 7 sites and their degrade arms are rows 3 and 4 of the table
   above; the refusal comment is quoted in point 4 below.

4. **A PM holder is not preempted away, so a hold is bounded.** On aarch64
   `manager()` masks interrupts for the lifetime of the guard. On x86 it does
   not, but the timer's dispatch path refuses to switch when it cannot get PM —
   `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:339-346`: *"Refusing here leaves the
   lock-holding context — the only one that can release it — running."* A CPU
   spinning in `manager()` therefore has a runnable holder ahead of it.
   claim-lint:ok: 2 of 2 dispatch entry paths refuse identically — that is what
   the quoted comment at `interrupts/context_switch.rs:339-346` is asserting, and
   the aarch64 twin is `context_switch.rs:6965`'s `TtbrResult::PmLockBusy` arm.

5. **Corollary, recorded because it decides the oracle's shape.** On a
   uniprocessor boot two *threads* cannot contend for PM at all: the holder
   cannot be preempted (point 4), so the second thread does not run. Real
   thread-vs-thread PM contention — and therefore #796 itself — needs a
   dispatching peer CPU. The two x86 gates this round runs
   (`docker/qemu/run-x86-boot-tests.sh:130`,
   `docker/qemu/run-x86-prod-profile-boot-test.sh`) boot `-smp 1`; the aarch64
   strict and production gates boot `-smp 4`.
   claim-lint:ok: the `-smp` values are the QEMU argv lines in those 2 of 2
   scripts, quoted in the oracle section below.

## STEP 2 — repair, per site

Rows 1-3 of census table (a) take the blocking `crate::process::manager()`.
Rows 4-6 are untouched.

### Row 1 — `sys_fcntl` (`kernel/src/syscall/handlers.rs`)

`try_manager()` + `Err(11)` becomes `crate::process::manager()`. The argument,
re-derived at these bytes and recorded in the comment that now sits at the
acquisition:

1. **The caller is preemptible thread context, entered from userspace.**
   `sys_fcntl` is reachable only through the syscall dispatcher, i.e. on a trap
   taken at EL0/ring 3. `aarch64/exception.rs:2331` takes the blocking
   `manager()` from the same context class for a CoW abort and states the same
   justification: a CPU that was running userspace when it trapped does not
   already own PROCESS_MANAGER, so this acquisition cannot self-deadlock.
2. **No asynchronous IRQ handler blocks on this lock** (census (c) point 3: 7 of
   7 asynchronous IRQ-context accesses are `try_manager()` with a
   refuse-and-re-arm degrade path), so waiting here cannot wedge an ISR.
3. **The wait is bounded by the holder's window** (census (c) point 4): a PM
   holder is not preempted away, on either architecture.
4. **This is already the discipline in this file.** `sys_dup` and `sys_dup2`,
   the two functions directly above `sys_fcntl`, mutate the same
   `process.fd_table` from the same context under a blocking
   `crate::process::manager()`. `sys_fcntl` was the outlier, not the precedent.
5. **No lock-order exposure.** The window takes no scheduler lock and does no
   I/O, so `SCHED_AFTER_PM_VIOLATIONS` / `[EXEC_LOCK_ORDER:VIOLATION:PM_HELD]`
   cannot fire from it.

Option (b) — bounded retry with a yield — was not used here, because the
conditions for (a) are met and (b) would leave the wrong errno reachable at the
end of the bound. There is no correct errno to return: POSIX has no "the kernel
was busy" answer for `F_SETFD`.

### Rows 2 and 3 — `sys_sigsuspend`, both architecture bodies (`kernel/src/syscall/signal.rs`)

Same change, same argument. Two details specific to these sites:

* The guard scope does not change. Both bodies already confined the PM window to
  a `{ }` block that closes before
  `with_scheduler(block_current_for_signal_with_context(...))`, so the repair
  does not create a PM-held scheduler acquisition.
* The inner "process manager not initialized" and "process not found" arms keep
  returning `ESRCH`. Those are about the manager being absent or the caller
  having no process row, not about lock contention, and they are out of this
  issue's scope.

### Rows 4-6 — `graphics.rs`, deliberately unchanged

`sys_fbinfo`, `sys_fbdraw` and `sys_fbmmap` are Breenix-private syscalls with no
POSIX name, so there is no errno list for `ErrorCode::Busy` to violate. Each
already spins (4096 or 65536 iterations) before giving up, and the comment at
`graphics.rs:2355` records why the fallback exists: a blocking acquisition there
can deadlock against the render thread. Changing them would be a regression.

## STEP 3 — the oracle

`fcntl_pm_contention_oracle`, a `boot_tests`-profile in-kernel oracle
(`kernel/src/test_framework/registry.rs`), registered in `SYSCALL_TESTS` at
`TestStage::ProcessContext`.

**What it does.** A kthread pinned to a live peer CPU takes PROCESS_MANAGER and
holds it for an 8000 us window measured on `CNTVCT_EL0`, with interrupts masked
for the length of the hold. The driving test thread waits for the window to
open, reads the lock's state independently, then issues 64
`fcntl(fd, F_SETFD, FD_CLOEXEC)` calls through the production `sys_fcntl` and
counts how many return `EAGAIN`.

**Marker.**

```
[FCNTL_PM_CONTENTION_ORACLE:aarch64:attempts=N:armed=1:holder_cpu=C:pm_busy_probe=1:calls=64:eagain=0:first_errno=9:first_wait_us=W:hold_done=1:joined=1:PASS]
```

**Anti-vacuity.** Two fields keep a green verdict out of reach without real
contention:

* `pm_busy_probe` is an independent try-lock read taken immediately before the
  measured call, so "contended" is a reading rather than an assumption about the
  peer's timing;
* `first_wait_us` is the measured duration of the first call, floored by the
  oracle at 1000 us of the 8000 us window, so a call that sailed through an
  uncontended lock scores FAIL.

**What `first_errno=9` means, stated rather than hidden.** The driving thread is
a kthread with no process row, so on a repaired kernel the call gets the lock
and then fails the process lookup with `EBADF`. The oracle measures *which arm
the syscall takes when the lock is busy*. It does not witness a successful
`F_SETFD`; `tty_oracle`'s `cloexec_exec` arm does that, on the production
profile, and it is unchanged by this branch.

**x86 arm.** `-smp 1`, where two threads do not contend for this lock at all
(census (c) point 5), so the oracle emits

```
[FCNTL_PM_CONTENTION_ORACLE:x86:arm=none:reason=uniprocessor_no_pm_contention_peer:online_cpus=1:SKIP]
```

returns false, and is emitted once from the marker-only stage path. This mirrors
`CENSUS_WIDEN_ORACLE`'s x86 SKIP, which discloses the same class of platform
limitation in the same shape.

### Three rounds of defects the oracle's own boots found

Recorded because each would have shipped a flaky gate, and each was found by
running the oracle rather than by reading it. Details are in the commit messages
of `eebb0c1f` and `c6a95555`; in summary:

1. Registered in `SYSCALL_TESTS` at `PostScheduler`, its window landed beside
   `census_widen_oracle`'s baseline (subsystem test threads run concurrently)
   and reddened it on 2 of 2 boots while plain `origin/main` scored 3 of 3.
2. Choosing the peer as "the first CPU that is not me" picked a CPU the boot had
   stopped dispatching on; the probe was migrated off it and bounced by the
   affinity retain check, giving `joined=0` on 3 of 3 boots. Fixed with
   `live_peer_cpu_for_test()` plus a `release_cpu_affine_thread_for_test()`
   before the exit wait.
3. A 3 ms window with a 5 ms timer tick let the driver sleep through the whole
   hold on 1 of 3 boots (`armed=0`), and holding the lock with interrupts
   enabled let the holder be descheduled between acquire and release, hanging
   1 of 6 boots outright at `[TEST:syscall:fcntl_pm_contention_oracle:START]`.
   Fixed by spinning rather than halting in the arm wait, widening the window to
   8 ms, masking interrupts for the hold, and bracketing the measured section in
   `preempt_disable`/`preempt_enable`.

The final home is `SYSCALL_TESTS` at `TestStage::ProcessContext`: stages are
barriers -- `advance_to_stage` joins each subsystem thread before returning --
so the window falls strictly after the `PostScheduler` verdicts.
`PROCESS_TESTS` was not available -- `tests/strand_handoff_structure.rs` ratchets
`census_widen_oracle` as the last registration in that array, and registering
before it would put a kthread-stack allocation inside the accounting window that
ratchet exists to protect.

## What is NOT claimed

* **#796 is not shown unable to recur through some other path.** What the
  oracle reddens on `origin/main` and greens here is the specific arm that
  produced the reported `EAGAIN`, and the census covers 18 of 18 try-lock sites
  in the scope it states. A site outside `kernel/src/syscall/` and the two
  architecture syscall entry points -- a helper reached from a syscall that
  turns its own try-lock failure into an errno -- would not have been found by
  the three greps recorded above.
* **The oracle does not witness a successful `F_SETFD` under contention.** Its
  driving thread has no process row, so its calls end at `EBADF` on a repaired
  kernel. It measures which arm the syscall takes when the lock is busy. The
  successful-`F_SETFD` witness is `tty_oracle`'s `cloexec_exec` arm on the
  production profile, which this branch leaves unchanged.
* **The x86 arm is not evidence about contention.** It is a disclosed SKIP:
  `-smp 1` does not produce two threads contending for this lock. The repair is
  arch-neutral, but 1 of the 2 rosters -- aarch64 -- tests it under real
  contention.
* **The production-profile `EAGAIN` rate is not measured.** The observed defect
  was 1 boot in 10 on main; 3 production boots here cannot distinguish that rate
  from 0. What supports the fix is the mechanism -- the arm that returned
  `EAGAIN` is deleted -- plus the oracle's deterministic red-to-green under
  forced contention, not a production-profile rate.
* **`sigsuspend`'s repair is not covered by the oracle.** Rows 2 and 3 take the
  same acquisition change under the same argument, and the aarch64 strict and
  production gates exercise signal paths, but no oracle in this branch forces
  contention at those two sites specifically.
* **No statement is made about the non-errno try-lock sites in census table
  (b).** Several of them skip work when the lock is busy (a signal check
  deferred to the next timer interrupt, a context save skipped). Whether any of
  those silent degradations is itself a defect is not investigated here.
