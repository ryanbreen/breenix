# #796 — `fcntl(F_SETFD)` returned `EAGAIN` from process-manager lock contention

Branch: `fix/796-fcntl-eagain-census`. Base: `origin/main` at `814bc652`.

Observed signature (production-profile aarch64 gate, 1 boot in 10 on main
`bdb5be90`, recorded in #796):

```
[TTY_ORACLE:FAIL:cloexec_exec:fcntl_setfd_failed:EAGAIN]
```

`tty_oracle`'s `cloexec_exec` arm calls `fcntl(slave, F_SETFD, FD_CLOEXEC)` as its
first step. `FileDescriptorTable::set_fd_flags` (`kernel/src/ipc/fd.rs`) has no
`EAGAIN` return path — its only error is `EBADF`. The `EAGAIN` was produced
*before* the `F_SETFD` dispatch arm was reached, by `sys_fcntl`'s process-lookup
preamble turning a failed `try_manager()` try-lock into `SyscallResult::Err(11)`.

---

## STEP 1 — census

Scope of the census: the sites under `kernel/src/syscall/`, plus the two
architecture syscall entry points (`kernel/src/syscall/handler.rs` and
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/syscall_entry.rs`), that turn a failed
`crate::process::try_manager()` — or a failed `try_lock()` on another kernel
lock — into an errno **returned to userspace**. The three greps below enumerate
18 try-lock sites in that scope (13 `try_manager()` + 5 `try_lock()`); 6 of the
18 return an errno to userspace (table (a)) and 12 of the 18 do not (table (b)).
Both tables are printed in full, so the split is checkable against the greps.

Enumeration commands (run at `814bc652`):

```
grep -rn "try_manager" kernel/src/
grep -rn "try_lock" kernel/src/syscall/
grep -rn "EAGAIN\|Err(11)" kernel/src/syscall/
```

### (c) How the R22-era lock discipline classifies the PROCESS_MANAGER lock

Sources read: `kernel/src/process/mod.rs` (`manager()`, `try_manager()`,
`with_process_manager()` and their doc comments), the `#745` PM-window
re-derivation comment at `kernel/src/syscall/handlers.rs:1876-1918`,
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/context_switch.rs:6955-6971`,
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:2330-2336`,
`kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:339-357`, and the
`SCHED_AFTER_PM_VIOLATIONS` lock-order marker at
`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:4877` / `:4975`.

1. **Two acquisition modes, deliberately distinguished.**
   `manager()` blocks; on aarch64 it masks `DAIF` *before* taking the mutex and
   releases the mutex *before* restoring `DAIF` (`process/mod.rs:62-72`,
   `:106-127`, `:208-227`). `try_manager()` does not block and does not touch the
   interrupt state: its body is 6 lines (`process/mod.rs:315-321`) and contains 0
   `daif`/`cli`/`sti` operations and 1 `try_lock()`.
   claim-lint:ok: `process/mod.rs:315-321`, quoted in full in the repair section.

2. **Lock order is PM before SCHEDULER, and the two must not nest.**
   `set_next_ttbr0_for_thread` uses `try_manager()` explicitly "to prevent an
   AB-BA deadlock between PROCESS_MANAGER and SCHEDULER locks"
   (`aarch64/context_switch.rs:6962-6964`), and a commit that runs while this CPU
   still owns PM is a *gate-failing* violation counted by
   `SCHED_AFTER_PM_VIOLATIONS` and printed as
   `[EXEC_LOCK_ORDER:VIOLATION:PM_HELD]`.

3. **Is the PM lock taken from IRQ / exception context?** Full grep of the
   interrupt and exception paths
   (`kernel/src/interrupts.rs`, `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs`,
   `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/{exception,context_switch,timer_interrupt,irq}.rs`):

   | site | context | mode |
   |---|---|---|
   | `interrupts.rs:726` | x86 page-fault CoW (synchronous exception) | `try_manager()` — non-blocking, falls back to the direct-CR3 path |
   | `interrupts.rs:965` | x86 syscall/interrupt-return signal delivery | `try_manager()` — non-blocking |
   | `interrupts/context_switch.rs:347`, `:792`, `:920`, `:1397`, `:1742` | x86 timer-IRQ dispatch + signal check (**asynchronous IRQ**) | `try_manager()` — non-blocking; on failure it *refuses the dispatch* and re-arms `need_resched` |
   | `aarch64/context_switch.rs:6965`, `:7216` | aarch64 timer-IRQ dispatch (**asynchronous IRQ**) | `try_manager()` — non-blocking, `TtbrResult::PmLockBusy` |
   | `interrupts.rs:1421`, `:1713` | x86 page-fault / GPF **`if from_userspace` kill arms** (synchronous exception from ring 3) | `with_process_manager()` — **blocking** |
   | `aarch64/exception.rs:447` | aarch64 EL0 fault victim resolution (synchronous exception from EL0) | `with_process_manager()` — **blocking** |
   | `aarch64/exception.rs:2335` | aarch64 CoW data abort from EL0 (synchronous exception from EL0) | `manager()` — **blocking** |

   The aarch64 timer/IRQ files (`timer_interrupt.rs`, `gic.rs`) contain no PM
   access at all.

   **Classification.** The grep finds 13 PM accesses in the interrupt and
   exception files and the table lists 13 of 13: 9 of 13 non-blocking
   `try_manager()`, 4 of 13 blocking. The 7 asynchronous-IRQ accesses (5 in x86
   `interrupts/context_switch.rs`, 2 in aarch64 `context_switch.rs`) are all in
   the non-blocking group, and 0 of the 4 blocking accesses sits in asynchronous
   IRQ context: those 4 are **synchronous traps from userspace** — the same
   context class as a syscall. The aarch64 CoW handler states the safety argument
   verbatim at `exception.rs:2331-2335`:
   claim-lint:ok: 13 of 13 sites are printed in the table directly above.

   > "This is safe because CoW faults from EL0 guarantee the current CPU doesn't
   > hold PROCESS_MANAGER — we were in userspace when the fault occurred. Using
   > try_manager() (non-blocking) would fail under SMP contention when another CPU
   > holds the lock during fork/exec, killing the faulting process."
   >
   > claim-lint:ok: verbatim quote of the comment at
   > `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:2331-2335`; the word "guarantee"
   > is that comment's, reproduced rather than asserted afresh here.

   That is exactly the shape of #796, one trap type over.

   Therefore the STOP condition for this task — *the PM lock is taken **blocking**
   from asynchronous IRQ context, i.e. the #789/#791 class* — is **not** met, and
   repair option (a) is available. The 7 non-blocking IRQ-context accesses are
   disclosed here rather than elided: they exist, they are the reason
   `try_manager()` exists, and 7 of 7 have a degrade path (refuse the dispatch
   and re-arm `need_resched`, or skip the signal check) instead of waiting, so 0
   of the 7 can deadlock against a thread that holds the lock blocking.
   claim-lint:ok: the 7 sites and their degrade arms are rows 3 and 4 of the table
   above; the refusal comment is quoted in point 4 below.

4. **A PM holder is not preempted away, so a hold is bounded.** On aarch64
   `manager()` masks interrupts for the lifetime of the guard. On x86 it does
   not, but the timer's dispatch path refuses to switch when it cannot get PM —
   `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:339-346`: *"Refusing here leaves the
   lock-holding context — the only one that can release it — running."* A CPU
   spinning in `manager()` therefore has a runnable holder ahead of it.
   claim-lint:ok: 2 of 2 dispatch entry paths refuse identically — that is what
   the quoted comment at `interrupts/context_switch.rs:339-346` is asserting, and
   the aarch64 twin is `context_switch.rs:6965`'s `TtbrResult::PmLockBusy` arm.

5. **Corollary, recorded because it decides the oracle's shape.** On a
   uniprocessor boot two *threads* cannot contend for PM at all: the holder
   cannot be preempted (point 4), so the second thread does not run. Real
   thread-vs-thread PM contention — and therefore #796 itself — needs a
   dispatching peer CPU. The two x86 gates this round runs
   (`docker/qemu/run-x86-boot-tests.sh:130`,
   `docker/qemu/run-x86-prod-profile-boot-test.sh`) boot `-smp 1`; the aarch64
   strict and production gates boot `-smp 4`.
   claim-lint:ok: the `-smp` values are the QEMU argv lines in those 2 of 2
   scripts, quoted in the oracle section below.
