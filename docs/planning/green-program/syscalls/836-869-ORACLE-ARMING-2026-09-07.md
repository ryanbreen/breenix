The injected same-CPU negative control reproduced the two-second timeout/cancel/late-acquire signature. It did **not** establish the mechanism of a natural #869 failure. The predeclared baseline control and loaded legs both passed ten boots; the requested several-baseline-red/zero-fixed-red load proof remains **unfulfilled**, and both baseline arm comparisons are **inconclusive**. Neither issue is closed by this report.

## Per-arm RCA and implementation

**#836:** Baseline R:4893 makes the holder runnable, but R:4908 pins the driver only after spawn returns. The hold starts at R:4794, ACTIVE publishes at R:4795, and the driver starts its arm clock at R:4916. A driver delayed in the exposed interval can miss most of the 250-ms safety interval before its measured wait even begins. The historical small-wait/safety-release signature is consistent with this guest scheduling route; it does not timestamp a host deschedule. `kthread_run_with` (`kernel/src/task/kthread.rs:140`) restores interrupts after publication before returning its handle. The source gap includes that return tail. No natural #836 red occurred in this round's baseline legs.

**#869:** Baseline peer selection (R:4865 and fallback R:4867) excludes the CPU executing that selection, not whichever CPU the driver later reaches before R:4908. Migration onto the selected peer followed by pinning can withhold the holder until cancellation/unpin/affinity cleanup. Baseline R:4772 tries the PM lock before R:4775 checks cancellation, so `acquired=1` after timeout is compatible with a holder first reaching the body after cancellation. R:4963 affinity cleanup can change where it runs. The natural aggregate signature does not distinguish this route from delayed distinct-peer delivery, generic kthread entry delay, host vCPU denial, or late acquisition/publication. The negative control below isolates invalid same-CPU placement, not the historical natural mechanism.

**Final lifetime:** `TestOracleDriverPin` is private and aarch64-only (final R:4847). `PhantomData<*mut ()>` prevents Send/Sync transfer of the per-CPU decrement. Constructor R:4851 masks only the existing preemption increment, including its per-CPU pointer lookup, then restores IRQs. Drop R:4863 calls the existing decrement. Fcntl enters at R:4900 before selection/fallback at R:4909/4911, spans spawn R:4923 and both measured call sites R:4954/4962, requests unconditional cancellation at R:4971, and drops at R:4973 before affinity cleanup R:4980 and join R:4982. The common tail at R:5027 handles no-peer/spawn-error before serial output. The ACTIVE reading, arm clock, failure priority, holder mask, and scored fields retain their meanings.

**Allocation audit:** The wider pin covers `kthread_run_with`'s String/Box/Arc allocation, `Thread::new_kernel` (`kernel/src/task/thread.rs:853`), `alloc_kernel_stack` (`kernel/src/memory/mod.rs:178`), and aarch64 stack allocation (`kernel/src/memory/kernel_stack.rs:779`). The heap wrapper (`kernel/src/memory/heap.rs:56`) masks during its free-list lock. Stack bitmap custody is released before live-slot checks and scrubbing; error cleanup returns the slot through the bitmap. `spawn_on_cpu_for_test` (`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:5534`) masks registry/queue publication and the reschedule IPI. The inspected allocation/error path has no required sleep/yield; it still takes locks and is not described as lock-free. Dispatch/per-CPU definitions and prohibited files were read-only.

**IRQ parity:** Packet and socket setup occurs before the guard. Final R:6094 pins before the single peer-selection match outside the retry loop. R:6108 repins at the next attempt, R:6138 drops on spawn failure, and R:6155 drops after arm-wait before affinity release/join. Common-tail release is R:6220. No IRQ_HOLD constant changed. This is structural hardening, **not an attributed #812 fix**. The existing balanced-lifetime test includes IRQ initial-selection/retry/cleanup checks without a tenth test or new file. A dedicated IRQ mutation campaign was not run. The peer remains selected once as specified: repinning a retry does not reselect the peer after migration during a previous cleanup, so this report does not claim distinct placement across such retries.

## Claims, limits, and follow-up

The executable fcntl pin lifetime and separate publication/selection mutation ratchets are established. The 40-boot comparison and induced observation are completed evidence, with the limits stated above. Specifically, this round does **not** claim:

- An unconditional repair for host vCPU starvation, or that host load/whole-process CPU seconds identify the cause of a particular red.

- A demonstrated reduction in natural #836/#869 failures. Both baseline arms were 0/10 under load, so the requested several-red/zero-red comparison is inconclusive and its acceptance target remains unfulfilled.

- An x86/beast result. There was no beast/x86 leg: both changed driver bodies and the shared helper are aarch64-only, and the x86 branches are unchanged. Production-profile verification is a regression sanity check, not a claim that this test-framework pin changes production dispatch.
