## Result and scope

The repair is in the **aarch64 oracle harness**, not the production TTY driver.
GDB reproduced the expired PM hold on the unchanged PR B kernel by scheduling
the peer to completion while the driver was stopped before its busy probe.
The driver then injected with a stale active flag and an unlocked mutex.
The foreground latency measurement also admitted timer IRQ execution inside
its measured interval. The repair uses a release acknowledgement, before/after
lock witnesses, a bounded safety failure, and a masked measurement matching
the level-triggered keyboard IRQ context. The 1000 microsecond ceiling remains.

The A/B did not reproduce the failure naturally. It does not establish that
PR B introduced it. The exact interrupt or host scheduling delay responsible
for the two historical foreground latency overruns is not identified.

## Mechanism and confirmation

The original harness is captured with original source line numbers in
`serials/959/original-harness-source.txt`, from `c93ecf52`.
Original `kernel/src/test_framework/registry.rs:6703` published PM active,
`:6708` ended the hold on elapsed time alone, and `:6715` dropped the guard.
Original `:6855` accepted the active flag before the independent busy probe;
active was not cleared at release. FG used the analogous fixed hold at
original `:7124`, and its driver probe was at original `:7300`.
Thus “the peer acquired earlier” did not establish “the peer still owns it
at the injection.” The hold needs to cover the IRQ-path window itself.

`serials/959/gdb-original/gdb.txt` records the controlled schedule:

- Driver stopped before PM busy probe: active=1, done=0, holder CPU=2.
- Only that holder CPU ran to completion: active=1, done=1, hold=20000 us.
- Driver resumed past its busy probe with the same stale active state.

`serials/959/gdb-original/serial.txt:664` then reports busy=0, blocking=0,
hold=20000 us, entry=50 us, joined=1, and FAIL:peer_hold. This is a GDB-driven
reproduction of the expired-hold mechanism, not an extra natural A/B failure.
The GDB script and ELF disassembly are included alongside the transcript.

For FG, `kernel/src/test_framework/registry.rs:7162` disables preemption
inside the injection helper, but does not itself mask interrupts. The old
caller also left interrupts enabled. The counter uses CNTVCT, so its elapsed
interval is not exclusive TTY execution time. In
`serials/959/gdb-fg-forced-irq-v2/gdb.txt`, a timer made pending through GDB
interrupts the original timed region at `tty_irq_fg_inject+184`, return PC
`0xffff00004056272c`, saved SPSR `0x60000005` (IRQ mask clear).
This confirms the contamination mechanism; it does not identify the historical
timer/softirq, or exclude a host scheduling contribution to 6670 us.
The first capture attempt had a host-side signed-address decoder error;
its transcript remains under `serials/959/gdb-fg-forced-irq/`.

The production input path reads the foreground snapshot at
`kernel/src/tty/driver.rs:326` and `:764`; its signal hop tries PM at `:852`.
The real aarch64 input dispatch is `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:2137`,
with nested IRQ reopening excluded for a level-triggered SPI at `:2229`.
The classification is in `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/gic.rs:890`.
The old thread-context oracle could instead run IRQ handling and softirq work
(`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:2252`) during its stopwatch interval.
These production files were inspected, not modified.

### What PR B changed, and what is not established

At HEAD, `kernel/src/socket/unix.rs:238` defers delivery from PM/IRQ/scheduler
scope to the owned intrusive pair queue at `:266`. The drain at `:281` is
called from `kernel/src/task/process_task.rs:1238`; actual Unix delivery
wakes readers and writers at `kernel/src/socket/unix.rs:253`.
`kernel/src/syscall/blocking_io.rs:115` now prepares a Unix writer wait at
`:137` and enters the shared prepared-wait helper at `:141`.
Those changes can move runnable-work publication and reclamation work relative
to a driver's fixed 20 ms window. They do not remove the TTY peers' explicit
mutex acquisitions, which are visible in the original source and GDB hold.

The two deferred mechanisms should not be conflated: Unix owned-pair close
work drains in thread context; pipe notification delivery at
`kernel/src/ipc/pipe.rs:46` calls `kernel/src/task/waitqueue.rs:182`, which
publishes through `isr_unblock_for_io` at `:185`. The latter two files have
no diff between the A/B revisions. Source inspection explains plausible
scheduling effects, but neither the A/B nor the available historical captures
establishes a PR B close notification as the trigger for these failures.
