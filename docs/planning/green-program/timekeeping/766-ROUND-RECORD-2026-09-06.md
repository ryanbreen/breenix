## 7. The review round, and the leg it withdrew

Review finding `unbounded-head-priority-for-rearming-timers` asked for the
cross-class fairness question to be analysed, bounded, named and tested. The
analysis, the bound and the naming are RCA section 2.1 (commit `e6085bdf`), and
they are what closes the finding. This section is what happened to the test.

**What was built.** `3a8c90f8` turned the leg into 8 CPU-bound peers plus 4
re-armers sleeping 8 times each, reported the worst of those 32 wake overruns as
`overrun_ms`, and added `peer_max_gap_ms`: the worst interval any peer spent off
the CPU during the window, measured by the peers themselves from consecutive
clock reads in their own spin loops. Both gates pinned the new fields.

**What it measured.** On x86 (`serials/766/10-rearm-leg-x86-three-runs.txt`):

```
[TIMER_WAKE_LATENCY_ORACLE:x86:sleep_ms=10:peers=8:rearmers=4:rearms=32:overrun_ms=45:bound_ms=100:quantum_ms=50:round_ms=400:peer_max_gap_ms=525:peer_gap_bound_ms=2800:wake_enqueues=33:peers_started=8:peers_spinning=8:backstops=0:setup_ms=744:window_ms=984:measured=1:PASS]
```

525 ms is the worst any of the 8 peers waited while 32 promotions went past it.
For scale: the 8 peers, the 4 re-armers and the boot thread are 13 runnable
threads, so a plain FIFO round in which each held a full 50 ms quantum would
leave a peer waiting 600 ms. On aarch64, 20 boots read `peer_max_gap_ms` 27 to
48 (median 31.5) and `overrun_ms` 7 to 16
(`serials/766/12-rearm-leg-aarch64-strict-20boot-oracle-lines.txt`). Those are
readings at one operating point, and section 8 says what they do not establish.

**Why it was withdrawn.** Three things, in the order they were hit:

1. Under mutation M2 the x86 gate failed with no marker in `serial_kernel.txt`,
   and the guest ran from the leg's own thread creation at guest `ms=383418` to
   `ms=990653` -- 606 s inside a leg whose longest budget is 30 s. That reading
   was taken from COM2 only. The marker is emitted on COM1, and that run's COM1
   was not preserved, so whether the leg printed a `FAIL` verdict or no marker
   at all cannot now be told from what is on disk. The work that followed was built
   on the weaker of the two readings, which is a mistake this record is naming
   rather than tidying away.
2. `8198ee9a` took the re-armers out of `kthread_park()` to keep them clear of
   an unbounded `kthread_join`, and put them on a `arch_halt_with_interrupts()`
   barrier wait instead. A halt leaves the thread in the ready queue. On a 50 ms
   quantum the x86 green gate then read
   `setup_ms=301074 ... backstops=10 ... rearms=8 ... FAIL` -- the same shape the
   oracle's own module doc records at 362 s for an earlier version of this leg.
   `bf097486` shrank the leg to 9 threads but kept the unparked barrier wait, so
   it carries the same defect and got 0 x86 runs.
3. The 12-thread leg scored 19/20 on the aarch64 strict gate on 4 runs of 4
   (serials 15 through 18), with a different filed signature each time: #869
   `arming_timeout`, #886 `irq_hold armed=0`, #886 fcntl safety-deadline, #826
   exec smoke. At the time the unchanged leg had scored 20/20 on 3 runs of 3,
   one of them an A/B taken on the same machine at a higher host load, and that
   contrast is what motivated the 9-thread resize.

**Point 3 did not hold up, and is retracted here.** The two runs of the
unchanged, shipped leg taken later the same afternoon (section 4) scored 19/20
each. On this host this afternoon the strict gate reds at a few percent whatever
this leg does, and the 0-of-60 versus 4-of-80 contrast was a draw, not a
mechanism. The resize commit `bf097486`'s message argues from that contrast and
is wrong about it; this record is the correction, and the commit is left in
history rather than rewritten.

What remains as reason to withdraw is points 1 and 2: the only x86-green variant
of the leg has no trustworthy red-on-mutation reading left on disk, and both
variants built after it fail on x86. A boot-test leg in that state is not
something to merge, and the round stopped iterating rather than start a fourth
variant. The measurement is on the record above; no gate reads it.

**Mutations run against the withdrawn leg** (recorded for completeness; 0 of 3
constrain the shipped tree): deleting
`:peer_max_gap_ms={}:peer_gap_bound_ms={}` from the marker's format string
reddened the ratchet at exit 101; replacing both `rearms=32` literals in

## 9. What is NOT claimed

* **Not claimed: that #766's own 324-trial distribution is reproduced.** This
  round measures one deterministic leg with a fixed peer count. It does not
  re-run the #693 stage-3 driver, and it does not re-derive p90 2592 ms.
* **Not claimed: that the detection term is removed.** The x86 timer interrupt
  handler still raises `need_resched` only at quantum expiry and still does not
  read the timer heap, so a due deadline can wait up to one quantum before any
  `schedule()` pass sees it. That term is inside the bound this oracle asserts
  and is deliberately left alone (RCA section 2 says why).
* **Not claimed: earliest-deadline order among wakes promoted in one pass.**
  The heap pops earliest-deadline-first and each pop goes to the front, so a
  pass promoting several reverses their order relative to one another.
* **Not claimed: that any gate reads a cross-class fairness quantity.** RCA
  section 2.1 derives a worst-case wait of `j*Q/(1-u)` for a CPU-bound thread
  against `j*Q` under a tail enqueue. Section 7's leg measured
  `peer_max_gap_ms` at one operating point on each arch and is withdrawn, so
  the shipped tree has no gate that would notice the promotion policy starving
  the CPU-bound class. That is a gap, and it is this round's, not a
  pre-existing one.
* **Not claimed: that the cross-class inflation factor is measured.** The two
  readings in section 7 are single points, not a sweep over re-armer counts or
  periods, and neither measures `u`.
* **Not claimed: that an aging or anti-starvation term is unnecessary.** This
  round adds 0 of them. The bound in RCA section 2.1 comes from the promoted thread having to
  run, not from a fairness mechanism, and a workload that needs a CPU-bound
  thread's ORDER protected rather than its share needs a policy change this
  round does not make.
* **Not claimed: that the aarch64 arm is evidence about the x86 mechanism.**
  With `MAX_CPUS = 8` the wake is routed to a least-loaded CPU, so neither the
  round-robin the fix removes nor the serialization step section 2.1 relies on
  applies there in the same way.
* **Not claimed: anything about #772.** A dispatched, already-woken reader that
  gives the CPU back without consuming its wake is a different mechanism, filed
  separately, and untouched here.
* **Not claimed: that #631 is fixed.** See section 4.
* **Not claimed: a boot-time saving.** The oracle ADDS work to the boot-test
  profile (9 kernel threads created, run and joined). `setup_ms` and `window_ms`
