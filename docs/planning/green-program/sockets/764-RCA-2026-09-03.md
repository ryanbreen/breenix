## Disposition

**Path: re-attribute, do not fix.** #764's mechanism is #766's mechanism,
observed through a different test. Nothing in the loopback data-wake path is
broken; the reader is woken correctly and promptly, then waits its turn on the
single ready queue #766 already measured. No kernel change is made by this
branch. `764-RCA-2026-09-03.md` (this file) plus the full evidence at
`serials/764-rca/README.md` are the disposition; `#764` and `#766` each carry
one comment, and the loopback test's own exit-13 message now prints the stamps
that make the next occurrence self-attribute (see "What changed in the test",
below).

**#764 stays OPEN as a Sockets-chip item only if the coordinator rules it so.**
This RCA's recommendation is re-attribution to the scheduler row: the defect
class it reproduces — a runnable thread waiting hundreds to thousands of
milliseconds for a dispatch turn under x86's single round-robin queue — is
`#766`'s, not a sockets defect. Closing #764 as a duplicate of #766, or
re-labeling it and leaving it open under the scheduler heading, are both
consistent with the evidence; this document does not choose between them and
neither issue is closed by this branch.

## The question

Issue #764: `loopback_wake_test_child` reader exits 13 —
`data_latency_ms=4740` against `DATA_WAKE_BOUND_MS=4000` — a data-path wake
bound distinct from #692's exit-15 EOF bound. #766, filed the same night from
#693's RCA, measured x86 `sleep_until` overruns of min 47 / p50 420 / p90 2592
/ max 10318 ms over 368 trials under the single round-robin queue (`MAX_CPUS =
1`, `TIME_QUANTUM = 10` ticks at `PIT_HZ = 200` = 50 ms slice, tail re-enqueue
at `scheduler.rs:3731`, ~24 live threads). The hypothesis to test, not assume:
is #764 that same dispatch-latency mechanism observed through a data wake, or
a distinct defect in the loopback/socket wake path?

## The answer, and how it was reached

The wake is neither lost nor late. `serials/707-2026-09-02/x86-battery-r2/boot01/serial_kernel.txt:2300`
puts the 16-byte payload in the receive buffer, `:2303` is `unblock(30)`
publishing the reader `Ready` (`scheduler.rs:2951`, reachable only after
`set_ready()` at `scheduler.rs:2897`), `:2304` is `Woke 1 connection waiters`,
and `:2305` is the writer's `sys_write` **returning** — the wake runs to
completion inside the writer's own syscall, before that syscall's return.
`grep -n 'unblock(30)'` returns exactly two lines for that whole boot (2210
accept, 2303 data): the reader is woken once, and nothing re-wakes it. That one
boot decides the first-order question and nothing more; it also shows the
reader dispatched seven times between the wake and its read (`Restored kernel
context for thread 30` at 2363/2489/2545/2656/2705/2749/2755, `woken from recv
blocking` at 2757) but cannot say why six of those seven turns made no
progress — a restore and a save at the same RIP look identical whether the
`still_blocked` check ran and read `Blocked`, or the thread executed nothing.

A 76-boot battery on this branch (`docker/qemu/run-x86-gate.sh 4 full`, beast
`breenix-x86`, KVM) reproduced exit 13 twice and stamped both:

- **`repro-boot-057`** (reader blocked in `recv`, `lat=5635` ms): the peer's
  whole write round trip is 345 ms (`write_ms=345`), and inside it the kernel
  serial shows received → `unblock(30)` → `Woke 1 connection waiters` →
  `sys_write` returns, the wake preceding the syscall return by two lines.
  Wake-to-run is the dominant term — 331 kernel-serial lines from the wake to
  the wait loop observing it, with six restores of the reader in between, the
  last of which consumes the wake — five wasted, one that made progress.
  `sys_read` returns one line after the wake is finally observed.
- **`repro-boot-046`** (`lat=4504` ms, **no blocking read**): the reader
  was still inside `accept()` when the peer wrote (`w0_to_pre=1346`); `grep -c
  'TCP recv: entering blocking path, thread=28'` returns 0. `pre_to_data=3158`
  ms elapsed between two adjacent syscalls of a runnable thread — a
  `clock_gettime` and a `read` whose data was already buffered (same grep
  above). This interval has no wake in it.

The mechanism (`serials/764-rca/README.md`, "The mechanism"): the loopback data
path wakes the reader synchronously inside the writer's own `sys_write`
(`wake_connection_waiters()`, `kernel/src/net/tcp.rs:1901`; `Scheduler::unblock()`,
`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:2884-2971`, setting `Ready` at `:2897` and
enqueuing at `:2951`). From that instant the reader is one runnable thread
among the boot's population (32 threads added, 22 `user: true` on
`repro-boot-057`) sharing **one** ready queue — x86 builds `MAX_CPUS = 1`
(`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:1012`) — round-robined by `Scheduler::schedule()`
(`scheduler.rs:1751`) with a 50 ms slice (`TIME_QUANTUM = 10` ticks at 200 Hz,
`kernel/src/interrupts/timer.rs:33`, reset per switch at
`kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:436`). One pass of that queue costs
hundreds of milliseconds of wall clock in the `testing,external_test_bins`
profile because the kernel's DEBUG-level serial logging costs roughly 10 ms a
line (measured at 9.1–11.3 ms/line on 36 of 46 boots carrying both a latency
figure and a line span, 9.1–18.5 on 43 of 46). `data_latency_ms` is the number
of queue passes the reader waits, times the cost of a pass — not a property of
the wake.

Three independent probes on this branch bracket the same 4000 ms bound with
dispatch delay alone, over the 76-boot battery: the load child's always-runnable
10 s spin loses the CPU for a median 1284 ms and up to 6782 ms (n=74); the
watchdog's timed sleep overruns its own deadline by up to 17388 ms (n=76) — the
same distribution #766 measured for `sleep_until` on x86. `data latency_ms`
itself: min 331, p50 1241, p90 2056, max 5635 (n=76). A 4000 ms bound sits
inside all three distributions, which is why #764 is rare (2/76 here, 1/25 in
the issue's own battery) rather than absent.

Left open, and not claimed settled here: why a given dispatch does not consume
the wake (five wasted of six restores on `repro-boot-057`, six wasted of seven
on the issue's own specimen); whether #692's exit-15 reds share this cause; whether
`DATA_WAKE_BOUND_MS` is the right bound.

## What changed in the test (userspace only, no kernel change)

`userspace/programs/src/loopback_wake_test.rs` (commits `cd9bff44`, `8a6840b4`
on this branch) now prints, from the same `monotonic_ms()` the bound is
measured against:

- `reader_stamps` — the peer's pre-write stamp, the reader's `accept()`
  return, its pre-read and post-read stamps, and the two derived gaps
  (`w0_to_pre`, `pre_to_data`) that tell an exit-13 apart as "blocked in
  `recv`" vs. "already runnable with data waiting", exactly as the two
  reproductions above differ.
- `peer_stamps` — the peer's connect, pre-write and post-write stamps and its
  own write round trip (`write_ms`).
- `reader_dispatch_probe` — called from the single call site inside the
  `data_latency_ms > DATA_WAKE_BOUND_MS` arm, so a boot that stays inside the
  bound does not execute it: a 2000 ms
  clock-sampling window whose largest inter-sample gap is a direct,
  wake-independent measurement of how long a runnable thread waited for a CPU
  on this boot, right after the bound was missed.

The wire shape is unchanged (`PAYLOAD_LEN` stays 16, the tag stays at `[8..]`,
the existing `data latency_ms=` line and exit code 13 are untouched), so the
gate signature this RCA investigates still reproduces as filed. The next
exit-13 will carry `reader_stamps`, `peer_stamps` and `reader_dispatch_probe`
in its own serial log and will not need a fresh RCA to tell whether it is this
same mechanism.
