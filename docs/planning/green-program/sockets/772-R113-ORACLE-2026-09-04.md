## 0. What the split measures, and where it is forced

A park primitive bumps a per-thread `wait_loop_iters` counter immediately
before the halt, so a thread's count advances once per counted park. The
dispatch mark stamps that count alongside the tid/RIP/RSP it installs
(`kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs`). At a save whose frame is
byte-identical to the mark, `classify_no_progress_kind`
(`kernel/src/per_cpu.rs`) compares the two:

| count vs stamp | kind | what it licenses |
|---|---|---|
| advanced | `REVISIT` | the thread reached a counted park point again between dispatch and save |
| unchanged | `ZERO_ITER` | the thread reached **no** counted park point between dispatch and save |
| identity mismatch or below stamp | `ITERS_UNKNOWN` | not attributable |

`ZERO_ITER` is therefore *not* a direct reading of "retired no instructions".
It is "did not park again through a counted primitive". The two coincide only
where the mark's RIP is a wait loop's own post-halt resume point, because the
only route back to that byte-identical RIP/RSP then runs through a primitive
that bumps.

### Where `ZERO_ITER` is the only representable answer — one reason family, not two

A thread resumed to ring 3 cannot reach a kernel park primitive before the
next interrupt, so its count cannot advance and an identical-frame save can
only land in `ZERO_ITER`. That covers `USER_PREEMPT` and `USER_MANDATORY`,
which the save-arm selection in `kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs`
picks on `from_userspace`. The counters agree in 6 of 6 boots: `USER_PREEMPT`
is 0 of 929 REVISIT on L1 and 0 of 551 on L0, and `USER_MANDATORY` has 0
identical frames on either arm.

**`KTHREAD_PREEMPT` is not in that class, and round 1 said it was.** Round 1's
record called it a reason "where `ZERO_ITER` is the only representable
answer". That is false about the code. `KthreadPreempt` is selected when a
kernel thread is saved while its state is neither blocked nor terminated, and
a kthread can be sitting on a counted park while Runnable.
`kernel/src/net/loopback_pump.rs` has exactly that shape in one loop body: it
blocks itself under `without_interrupts`, then **unblocks itself in the same
critical section** when the loopback queue turns out to be non-empty, then
yields, then parks on `crate::arch_halt_with_interrupts()` — Runnable, on a
counted primitive. A timer interrupt there takes the `KthreadPreempt` arm, and
a second one at a byte-identical frame after another pass through that halt
would read `REVISIT`. Round 2 widens the class further: `kthread_park`,
`kthread_join` and `WorkItem::wait` park on `crate::arch_halt`, which now
bumps too.

So this round's `KTHREAD_PREEMPT` REVISIT count — **0 of 63 on L1 and 0 of 17
on L0, 6 of 6 boots** — is a measurement, not a structural impossibility. The
representable-and-unobserved reading is the correct one.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: 2 of the 6 save reasons (USER_PREEMPT, USER_MANDATORY) are selected on from_userspace in kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs, read in this slot -->
The consequence for the aggregate: 992 of L1's 1634 `ZERO_ITER` (60.7%) and
568 of L0's 850 (66.8%) come from `USER_PREEMPT` plus `KTHREAD_PREEMPT`
together, of which the `USER_PREEMPT` part (929 on L1, 551 on L0) is
structurally forced and the `KTHREAD_PREEMPT` part (63 on L1, 17 on L0) is
not. **The whole-population zero-iteration headline is therefore mostly an

## Q3 — which reading the result supports

**A mixture, and the mixture is reason-dependent. Stated as the counters state
it, with the round-2 instrument:**

1. **The DIAG's revisit reading is real and is a large minority of the
   mandatory arm.** 365 of 865 L1 mandatory-arm identical frames (42.2%) and
   180 of 451 on L0 (39.9%) carry an advanced park count. Those dispatches did
   run their wait loop and re-park; that is a direct reading, not an inference
   from where a save lands. Over the three boots of each arm the share sits
   inside 36.8–48.1% (L1) and 34.8–42.9% (L0).
2. **The revisit reading is the near-total reading in exactly one reason, and
   it is not the one the DIAG argued about.** `KthreadMandatory` is 79 of 80
   REVISIT on L1 (98.8%) and 97 of 97 on L0 (100.0%). This is where round 1's
   coverage gap was concentrated: those threads park on `crate::arch_halt`,
   which round 1 did not count (Appendix A).
3. **The zero-iteration reading is still the majority of the mandatory arm,
   conditionally.** 500 of 865 (57.8%) on L1 and 271 of 451 (60.1%) on L0
   reached no counted park point between the dispatch and the save. **"No
   counted park" and "no completed loop iteration" coincide only where the
   mark's RIP is that wait loop's own post-halt resume point** — the condition
   Section 0 states, and it is not discharged here: the census reads counters
   only and does **not** tag any observation with a RIP, so the RIP of the
   `ZERO_ITER` marks specifically is unmeasured in these 6 boots. The
   `DISPATCH_SAVE` trace event in `kernel/src/tracing/providers/sched.rs` does
   carry the save's RIP, which for an identical-frame observation is the mark's
   RIP by construction — but these boots read counters over GDB and did not
   dump the trace buffer. What is available instead is the whole-serial RIP
   shape, which is over save records rather than over the `ZERO_ITER` subset:
   see Limits.
4. **The whole-population figure overstates the zero-iteration reading, mostly
   for a structural reason.** 1634 of 2081 L1 identical frames are ZERO_ITER
   (78.5%), but 992 of those 1634 (60.7%) come from `USER_PREEMPT` and
   `KTHREAD_PREEMPT` — of which the 929 `USER_PREEMPT` observations are
   structurally forced and the 63 `KTHREAD_PREEMPT` ones are not (Section 0).
   Read the mandatory arm, not the aggregate.
5. **The split is not diluted by unattributable observations.**
   `ITERS_UNKNOWN` is 0 of 3210 identical-frame observations across 6 of 6
   boots, so the mark's tid identity check held on every observation these
   boots took, and no observation was folded into either answer to make the
   partition close. The partition itself closes exactly: 54 of 54 checks
   (6 boots × 6 reasons, plus 6 boots × 3 aggregates) hold with 0 violations,
   recomputed in this slot from the committed JSON. The park side is audited
   too: `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_SKIPPED` reads 0 in 6 of 6 boots against 3408 parks
   total, so no park in this battery failed to reach a thread.

So the DIAG sentence under test — "the second reading is what the data shows"
— is right about the mechanism existing and wrong as a description of the
population it was applied to. It was argued from a *different* population than
the one it characterised: the DIAG's 186 of 226 evidence is over
frame-**changed** pairs (where a save lands), and its alternation specimen is
one thread; R113 instruments the frame-**identical** population directly and
finds the revisit mechanism accounting for about two in five of it on the
mandatory arm, and for essentially all of it on the kthread-mandatory arm.

The correction runs the other way too: the original zero-instruction reading is
not refuted, it is bounded, and the bound tightened between rounds. It
describes 500 of 865 (L1) and 271 of 451 (L0) mandatory-arm identical frames,
not all of them.

## Limits of this record

* **3 boots per arm.** Each arm-to-arm and round-to-round difference above is 3
  observations against 3. Per-boot minima and maxima are printed for the
  quantities the conclusions rest on; no p-value is computed, because the boot
  is the independent unit and n=3 does not support one.
* **The census does not tag an observation with its RIP.** The `DISPATCH_SAVE`
  trace event carries the kind in bits 5–6 and the save's RIP, but these boots
  read the 41 counter symbols each build's ELF carries over GDB and did not
  dump the trace buffer, so the fraction of `ZERO_ITER` saves sitting at a
  symbolised wait site is not measured here. Q3 item 3 keeps that condition
  explicit rather than asserting the coincidence. What is available is the
  whole-serial RIP shape, over save records rather than over the `ZERO_ITER`
  subset, and it has the same two-address form the DIAG symbolised: in
  `docs/planning/green-program/sockets/serials/772-r113-r2/L1/boot_01/serial_kernel.txt`
  there are 1021 `RIP=` records over 34 distinct addresses, 924 of them (90.5%)
  at two — `0x800023d5d2` (836 records) and `0x800023e739` (88 records). The
  mapping from those to the DIAG's two symbolised addresses is not re-derived
  here; the binaries differ.
  <!-- claim-lint:ok: 6 of 6 uncounted enable_and_hlt sites are idle or terminal halt loops, counted by grep in this slot -->
* **Six raw `enable_and_hlt` park sites are still uncounted**, and the
  bare-halt-instruction family beyond them (12 x86 sites, 15 aarch64), as
  enumerated above. A thread saved at a byte-identical frame while parked in
  one of them reads `ZERO_ITER` although it re-parked. The idle thread is the
  only thread that reaches the six, and 0 of the 6 and 0 of the 27 is a wait
  loop waiting on a condition, so the expected effect on the mandatory-arm
  figures is small — but it is not measured, and it biases in the same
  direction round 1's larger gap did.
* **A wrong-thread pointer at the PARK is unrepresentable.** Identity is
  re-checked at the save (`classify_no_progress_kind` compares the per-CPU
  current thread against the thread being saved and reports `ITERS_UNKNOWN` on
  a mismatch), but the park bumps whatever thread the per-CPU pointer names at
  that instant, with no identity to check it against — a park has no second
  source of truth for "who is running". If that pointer ever named a thread
  other than the one parking, the parking thread's next identical-frame save
  would read `ZERO_ITER` and some other thread's would read `REVISIT`, and
  nothing in this census would show it. `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_SKIPPED` does not close
  this: it separates "no thread installed" and "per-CPU not initialised" from
  an attributed park, not "the wrong thread was installed". It read 0 of 3408
  parks in 6 of 6 boots, and `ITERS_UNKNOWN` read 0 of 3210 identical-frame
  observations, which bounds the mismatch rate at the SAVE only.
* **In the 6 boots measured here, `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_TOTAL` and
  `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_SKIPPED` were both whole-machine atomics**, not per-CPU
  `TraceCounter`s, so they carry no `_CPU0` suffix in these boots'
  `gdb_output.txt` and are not directly comparable per-CPU with the
  `DISPATCH_*` values. On a single-CPU boot, which is what these 6 boots are,
  that distinction does not change any ratio above. The round-3 fix round
  changed the total: it is now a per-CPU `TraceCounter` incremented after the
  park path's `PER_CPU_INITIALIZED` guard, read by the driver as a sum over
  the per-CPU slots, and it therefore no longer counts a park that guard
  refuses (`WAIT_LOOP_PARK_SKIPPED`, still whole-machine, does). A future
  battery's totals are that quantity, not this one; the two coincide whenever
  SKIPPED is 0, which is what all 6 of these boots read.
* **x86 only.** aarch64 carries the park count on the same primitives but has
  no dispatch mark, so the split does not exist there this round.
* **`USER_MANDATORY` is empty.** 0 identical frames of 66 saves on each arm
  (22 per boot in 6 of 6 boots), so that reason contributes nothing to any
  ratio above.
* **The L0 arm has no serial/counter cross-check.**
  `kernel_blocked_saves_match_records` is present and `true` in 3 of 3 L1 boots
  and absent in 3 of 3 L0 boots, because the census script emits it only when
  `saved_records` is non-zero and `quiet_dispatch_log` suppresses the serial
  line it counts. L0's counter values therefore stand on the GDB read alone.
  <!-- claim-lint:ok: the sentence quoted here was read in kernel/src/lib.rs at 57c5f560 and is changed at HEAD; the count it should carry is 6 of 6 -->
* **One known-stale numeral in the instrument at the measured commit, fixed
  after it.** At `57c5f560` — the commit these 6 boots were measured on —
  `kernel/src/lib.rs`'s park-census doc listed **six** uncounted raw
  `enable_and_hlt` loops and then said "None of the five is a blocking wait
  loop waiting on a condition", a residue of the round-2 correction that
  raised that count from five to six. The round-3 fix round corrected it, and
  widened the same census to enumerate the bare-halt family as well. Nothing
  in this record was read off that comment — the census here is re-derived by
  grep — so no number above moves.
