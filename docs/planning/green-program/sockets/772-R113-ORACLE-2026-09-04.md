## 0. What the split measures, and where it is forced

`arch_halt_with_interrupts()` (`kernel/src/lib.rs:412`) bumps a per-thread
`wait_loop_iters` counter immediately before the halt, so a thread's count
advances once per park. The dispatch mark stamps that count alongside the
tid/RIP/RSP it installs (`kernel/src/interrupts/context_switch.rs:515-528`).
At a save whose frame is byte-identical to the mark,
`classify_no_progress_kind` (`kernel/src/per_cpu.rs:1246`) compares the two:

| count vs stamp | kind | what it licenses |
|---|---|---|
| advanced | `REVISIT` | the thread reached a park point again between dispatch and save |
| unchanged | `ZERO_ITER` | the thread reached **no** park point between dispatch and save |
| identity mismatch or below stamp | `ITERS_UNKNOWN` | not attributable |

`ZERO_ITER` is therefore *not* a direct reading of "retired no
instructions". It is "did not park again". The two coincide only where the
mark's RIP is a wait loop's own post-halt resume point, because the only
route back to that byte-identical RIP/RSP runs through
`arch_halt_with_interrupts()`, which bumps.

**That distinction is load-bearing, and it forces the classification in two
reasons.** A user thread resumed to ring 3 and a kthread preempted inside
its own code do not reach `arch_halt_with_interrupts()`, so their count
cannot advance and an identical-frame save can only land in `ZERO_ITER`. The
counters show that in 6 of 6 boots:

| arm | reason | identical-frame n | REVISIT | ZERO_ITER |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | `USER_PREEMPT` | 877 | 0 | 877 |
| L1 | `USER_MANDATORY` | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| L1 | `KTHREAD_PREEMPT` | 7 | 0 | 7 |
| L0 | `USER_PREEMPT` | 624 | 0 | 624 |
| L0 | `USER_MANDATORY` | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| L0 | `KTHREAD_PREEMPT` | 25 | 0 | 25 |

Every REVISIT observation in both arms falls in the three parking-capable
reasons (`KernelBlockedPreempt`, `KernelBlockedMandatory`,
`KthreadMandatory`): 317 of 317 on L1, 223 of 223 on L0, 0 outside them.
Conversely 884 of L1's 1407 `ZERO_ITER` (62.8%) and 649 of L0's 939 (69.1%)
come from the two reasons where `ZERO_ITER` is the only representable
answer. **The whole-population "81.6% zero-iteration" headline is therefore
mostly an artefact of which threads can park**, and the informative figure is
the parking-capable subtotal, printed alongside it throughout.

## Q3 — which reading the result supports

**A mixture, and the mixture is reason-dependent. Stated as the counters
state it:**

1. **The DIAG's revisit reading is real and is a minority of the mandatory
   arm.** 236 of 631 L1 mandatory-arm identical frames (37.4%) and 135 of 365
   on L0 (37.0%) carry an advanced park count. Those dispatches did run their
   wait loop and re-park; that is a direct reading, not an inference from
   where a save lands. Two boots per arm apart, the share sits inside
   35.2-40.5% (L1) and 36.6-37.2% (L0).
2. **The DIAG's revisit reading is the majority reading in exactly one
   reason, and it is not the one the DIAG argued about.** `KthreadMandatory`
   is 75 of 92 REVISIT on L1 (81.5%) and 84 of 99 on L0 (84.8%).
3. **The zero-iteration reading is the majority of the mandatory arm.** 395
   of 631 (62.6%) on L1 and 230 of 365 (63.0%) on L0 reached no park point
   between the dispatch and the save. In this population the mark's RIP is
   the wait loop's own post-halt resume point, so "no park" and "no completed
   loop iteration" coincide.
4. **The whole-population figure overstates the zero-iteration reading for a
   structural reason, not an empirical one.** 1407 of 1724 L1 identical
   frames are ZERO_ITER (81.6%), but 884 of those 1407 (62.8%) come from
   `USER_PREEMPT` and `KTHREAD_PREEMPT`, where REVISIT is not representable
   (section 0). Read the mandatory arm, not the aggregate.
5. **The split is not diluted by unattributable observations.**
   `ITERS_UNKNOWN` is 0 of 2886 identical-frame observations across 6 of 6
   boots, so the mark's tid identity check held on every observation these
   boots took, and no observation was folded into either answer to make the
   partition close. The partition itself closes exactly: 54 of 54 checks
   (6 boots × 6 reasons, plus 6 boots × 3 aggregates) hold with 0 violations.

So the DIAG sentence under test — "the second reading is what the data
shows" — is right about the mechanism existing and wrong as a description of
the population it was applied to. It was argued from a *different* population
than the one it characterised: the DIAG's 186 of 226 evidence is over
frame-**changed** pairs (where a save lands), and its alternation specimen is
one thread; R113 instruments the frame-**identical** population directly and
finds the revisit mechanism accounting for about three in eight of it on the
mandatory arm.

The corresponding correction runs the other way too: the original
zero-instruction reading is not refuted, it is bounded. It describes 395 of
631 (L1) and 230 of 365 (L0) mandatory-arm identical frames, not all of them.

## Limits of this record

* **3 boots per arm.** Each arm-to-arm difference above is 3 observations
  against 3. Per-boot ranges are printed for the quantities the conclusions
  rest on; no p-value is computed, because the boot is the independent unit
  and n=3 does not support one.
* **The census does not tag an observation with its RIP.** The
  `DISPATCH_SAVE` trace event carries the kind in bits 5-6 and the RIP, but
  these boots read counters over GDB and did not dump the trace buffer, so
  the fraction of `ZERO_ITER` saves sitting at a symbolised wait site is not
  measured here. What is available is the whole-serial RIP shape, and it has
  the same two-address form the DIAG symbolised: in
  `serials/772-r113/L1/boot_01/serial_kernel.txt` there are 857 `RIP=`
  records over 35 distinct addresses, 746 of them (87.0%) at two —
  `0x80002032c2` (614) and `0x8000204429` (132). The mapping from those to
  the DIAG's `…1823a2` / `…184d79` is not re-derived here; the binaries
  differ.
* **`note_wait_loop_park` is silent when the per-CPU current-thread pointer
  is null** in the build these 6 boots measured — it returned without bumping
  and without counting the skip. A park taken with no thread installed makes a
  later identical-frame save read `ZERO_ITER` when the thread had in fact
  parked. There was no counter for that path in the instrument these numbers
  came from, so its frequency is unmeasured HERE. The round-2 fix round added
  `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_TOTAL` and `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_SKIPPED`
  (`kernel/src/tracing/providers/counters.rs`) so a later battery can measure
  it; that battery is not this one, and the two instruments are not
  interchangeable, because the same round also widened the set of park
  primitives that bump the count.
* **A wrong-thread pointer at the PARK is unrepresentable.** Identity is
  re-checked at the save (`classify_no_progress_kind` compares the per-CPU
  current thread against the thread being saved and reports `ITERS_UNKNOWN`
  on a mismatch), but the park bumps whatever thread the per-CPU pointer names
  at that instant, with no identity to check it against — a park has no second
  source of truth for "who is running". If that pointer ever named a thread
  other than the one parking, the parking thread's next identical-frame save
  would read `ZERO_ITER` (its count did not move) and some other thread's
  would read `REVISIT`, and nothing in this census would show it. The
  `WAIT_LOOP_PARK_SKIPPED` counter added in round 2 does not close this: it
  separates "no thread installed" and "per-CPU not initialised" from an
  attributed park, not "the wrong thread was installed". `ITERS_UNKNOWN` read
  0 of 2886 identical-frame observations, which bounds the mismatch rate at
  the SAVE only.
* **x86 only.** aarch64 carries the park count but has no dispatch mark, so
  the split does not exist there this round.
* **`USER_MANDATORY` is empty.** 0 identical frames of 66 saves on each arm
  (22 per boot in 6 of 6 boots), so that reason contributes nothing to any
  ratio above.
* **The measure commit was not on `origin` when this record was written.**
  `da60cc6e` existed only in the beast scratch clone `/root/breenix-r113`;
  `origin/exp/772-r113-oracle` was at `a715d0f8`. It was recovered into this
  worktree over a git bundle and is pushed with this record. The
  `census.json` bytes read here are `da60cc6e`'s.

## Reproduction

From a checkout of this branch, with no host access:

```
python3 - <<'PY'
import json, glob
for p in sorted(glob.glob("docs/planning/green-program/sockets/serials/772-r113/*/boot_*/census.json")):
    c = json.load(open(p))["counters"]
    r = "KERNEL_BLOCKED_MANDATORY"
    print(p, c["DISPATCH_NOPROGRESS_REVISIT_%s_CPU0" % r],
          c["DISPATCH_NOPROGRESS_ZERO_ITER_%s_CPU0" % r],
          c["DISPATCH_NOPROGRESS_ITERS_UNKNOWN_%s_CPU0" % r],
          c["DISPATCH_NOPROGRESS_SAVE_%s_CPU0" % r])
PY
```
