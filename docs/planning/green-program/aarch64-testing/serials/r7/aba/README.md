# Round-7 A/B/A probe batteries — the serials the RCA's rows were counted from

Round 7 reported its two mutation results as bare rows in
`786-RCA-2026-09-04.md` with no committed serials behind them. The round-6
review (R7-006, R7-008) called that out. The serials were still on the host
that ran the RCA (`/tmp/breenix_aarch64_strict_failures/`, written by
`docker/qemu/run-aarch64-boot-test-strict.sh`), so round 8 commits them here.
The A/B/A numbers in the RCA are now this directory's own arithmetic.
claim-lint:ok: 6 numbers restated or backed here -- 13/26, 19/26, 0/24, 4/6,
0/18 and 4/8 -- each reproduced by the commands below.

## What each directory is

| directory | boots | what the kernel was |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline-26/` | 26 | branch head + diagnostic probes, no mutation |
| `mutation-a-6/` | 6 | + mutation A (the two shadow stores after the raw `msr`) |
| `mutation-ab-18/` | 18 | + mutation A and mutation B (the unreadable-identity arm returns `false`) |
| `reverted-8/` | 8 | both mutations removed again — the A/B/A reversion control |
| `earlier-unprobed-6/` | 6 | an earlier probe build, NOT part of the 26 (see below) |

`diffs/` holds the two patches the batteries were built from, exactly as the
RCA session reverted them out of its worktree:
`diagnostic-instrumentation-and-mutations.diff` and
`none-classification-probe.diff`.

## The counts, and the command that produces them

`CLASSIFICATION.tsv` is generated by grepping each committed serial; the
summary below is `awk` over that file. Both commands are reproducible against
this directory:

```
$ awk -F'\t' 'NR>1{n[$1]++; if($3>0) a[$1]++; if($4>0) w[$1]++} \
    END{for(b in n) printf "%-20s boots=%2d abort=%2d wedge=%2d\n", b, n[b], a[b]+0, w[b]+0}' \
    CLASSIFICATION.tsv
reverted-8           boots= 8 abort= 4 wedge= 7
baseline-26          boots=26 abort=13 wedge=19
mutation-a-6         boots= 6 abort= 0 wedge= 4
earlier-unprobed-6   boots= 6 abort= 3 wedge= 6
mutation-ab-18       boots=18 abort= 0 wedge= 0
```

`abort` counts serials containing `INSTRUCTION_ABORT] FAR`; `wedge` counts
serials containing `block_wedge_oracle:FAIL`. A boot showing both is counted in
both columns, which is why the columns do not sum to the boot count.

So, re-derived from committed bytes:

* instruction abort: **13 of 26** baseline, **0 of 24** with mutation A
  (`mutation-a-6` + `mutation-ab-18`), **4 of 8** on reversion.
* block-wedge refusal: **19 of 26** baseline, **4 of 6** with mutation A alone,
  **0 of 18** with both, **7 of 8** on reversion.

claim-lint:ok: the five rows above are the `awk` output pasted verbatim from
this directory's `CLASSIFICATION.tsv`.

## How the batteries were told apart

Timestamps group the boots into contiguous run windows. Two facts make the
grouping checkable rather than asserted:

1. **Mutation A leaves a per-boot stamp.** The pre-ERET probe prints the two
   per-CPU shadows at the instant before init's ERET. 24 of the 24 boots in
   `mutation-a-6` + `mutation-ab-18` read `next_cr3=0x0`; of the 20
   `baseline-26` boots that carry the probe at all, 16 read
   `next_cr3=0x40200000` — the kernel root, still armed:
   claim-lint:ok: 24 of 24 and 16 of 20, the two greps in this section.

   ```
   $ for f in baseline-26/*.txt; do grep -ao "RCA786_PRE_ERET] cpu=[0-9] next_cr3=0x[0-9a-f]* saved_proc_cr3=0x[0-9a-f]*" $f | head -1; done | sed 's/.*cpu=[0-9] //' | sort | uniq -c
      3 next_cr3=0x0 saved_proc_cr3=0x0
      1 next_cr3=0x0 saved_proc_cr3=0x100004406c000
     13 next_cr3=0x40200000 saved_proc_cr3=0x0
      3 next_cr3=0x40200000 saved_proc_cr3=0x100004406c000
   ```

   (6 of the 26 baseline boots come from the sub-battery that predates the
   pre-ERET probe, so 20 rows, not 26.)

2. **The 26 is the only grouping that reproduces round 7's rows.** Five
   contiguous windows survive on the host: 6 unprobed boots (3 aborts, 6
   wedges), 6 boots (5, 5), 8 boots (4, 6), 6 boots (2, 4), 6 boots (2, 4).
   Round 7 said its baseline was `6+8+6+6`. Of the four ways to pick a 6+8+6+6
   set out of those five windows, exactly one sums to 13 aborts and 19 wedges,
   and that is the set committed as `baseline-26/`. The window it leaves out is
   committed as `earlier-unprobed-6/` rather than dropped.

claim-lint:ok: 4 candidate 6+8+6+6 groupings enumerated, 1 sums to (13, 19).

**Mutation B carries no such per-boot stamp.** Its presence in
`mutation-ab-18/` rests on the run order the RCA recorded, not on a marker in
the serial — the mutation's whole effect is that a refusal does not happen, and
an absence cannot be stamped. What the committed bytes do show is that those 18
boots contain 0 `block_wedge_oracle:FAIL` lines while the 6 boots either side of
them (`mutation-a-6`, then `reverted-8`) contain 4 and 7.
