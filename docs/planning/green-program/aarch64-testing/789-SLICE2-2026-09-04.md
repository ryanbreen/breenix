# #789 slice 2 — scheduler-lock IRQ shape

Slice 2 masks the scheduler-lock acquisitions that ruling R153 found reachable
with interrupts enabled, deletes a dead one, and adds a structural ratchet over
the acquisition surface. The tracked defect is #790.

Review round 1 changed several claims in this document. What that round found,
what was repaired, and what is disclosed rather than repaired is in
"Review round 1" at the end; the sections above it are written as they now
stand, not as they first read.

## What changed

`crate::task::scheduler::is_current_idle_thread` — a free function in
`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs`, not a method on `Scheduler`, which is what round 3
corrected — keeps its `Option<bool>` contract and its non-blocking `try_lock`, but
now masks local interrupts before acquisition and restores the prior state after
the guard is dropped. `try_schedule` had no live caller on `origin/main`; it and
its `#[allow(dead_code)]` annotation were deleted.

The census also found three acquisitions outside a local mask that could not be
derived from an interrupt or exception entry: `init`, `init_with_current`, and
the callerless `try_liveness_snapshot`. Their complete guard lifetimes now use
the same arch-neutral mask.

Masking is deliberately the conservative choice. AArch64 has a lock-free
`CPU_IS_IDLE` scheduling flag (`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs`, `#[cfg(target_arch
= "aarch64")]`), but x86_64 does not share that representation, and extending
lock-free identity publication would mean auditing the context-switch
publication sites. `arch_without_interrupts` already saves and restores IF on
x86_64 and DAIF on AArch64. Masking before acquisition removes same-CPU IRQ
re-entry for the whole held window while the scheduler lock continues to
serialize peer CPUs, and restoring the saved state keeps nested use correct when
interrupts were already masked.

Round 1 also rewrote five doc comments in `kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs`.
The comment on `is_current_idle_thread` had said the `try_lock` was what made it
"safe to call from IRQ context" and what avoided deadlock — the exact belief
R153 demolished — and the repair had left it in place.

### The second repair the mask delivers

Round 3's review asked what else the mask buys, and there is a concrete second
answer that this document had not stated. `is_current_idle_thread` computes its
answer as `current_thread_id_inner().map(|id| id == idle_thread_id())`, and those
two accessors each index `self.cpu_state[Self::current_cpu_id()]`
(`kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs`) — the CPU index is read twice, independently,
once per accessor. With interrupts enabled, a preemption between the two reads
that resumes the thread on a different CPU would compare CPU A's current thread
against CPU B's idle thread: an answer about a pair that did not coexist, and one
the scheduler lock does not prevent, since holding the lock does not pin the
holder to a CPU. Masked, that window does not exist, because no preemption can
land between the reads. This is a window closed by construction; no such
mis-answer has been observed, and no stronger statement is made. The same paragraph is now in
the function's doc comment so the reason survives without this document.

## The acquisition surface is not uniform

The ratchet's own census, printed by the test under `--nocapture` and recorded at
`docs/planning/green-program/aarch64-testing/789-slice2/confirm/ratchet-postfix-2026-09-04.txt`:

```text
totals: 28 acquisition sites in 27 functions; 22 admitted by a local mask shape,
6 by caller derivation, 0 unadmitted
```

The 6 admitted by caller derivation rather than by a local mask are
`lock_scheduler` and `try_lock_scheduler` (the primitives themselves),
`lock_for_context_switch`, `switch_to_idle_best_effort`,
`terminate_thread_best_effort`, and `try_dump_state`.

The first five are interrupt or exception paths by design. `try_dump_state` is
the one worth naming: it is a `pub` diagnostic carrying the same shape this
slice set out to remove — `try_lock_scheduler()` with no mask — and it is
admitted only because both of its callers today
(`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/timer_interrupt.rs` and
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs`) are interrupt or exception context.
It was left unmasked on purpose: it allocates two `Vec`s and walks the thread
table while holding the guard, so masking it would put an unbounded allocating
walk inside a no-interrupt window, which is the trade `release_reclaimed_threads`
already declines on x86_64. Its doc comment now says so, and says that a
thread-context caller reddens the ratchet.

## Structural ratchet

`tests/teardown_structure.rs::scheduler_lock_acquisitions_are_irq_safe_by_shape`
censuses each Rust function below `kernel/src` whose body directly calls raw
`SCHEDULER.lock()` or `SCHEDULER.try_lock()`, derives the scheduler helper
spellings from those raw sites, and censuses their callers. A site is admitted
by shape when it is:

- lexically inside a `without_interrupts` or `arch_without_interrupts` closure;
- lexically inside the false arm of an `if` on a value read from `are_enabled`;
- after an architectural interrupt disable that DOMINATES it — see below; or
- reached only through callers satisfying one of those shapes, or bottoming out
  at an interrupt/exception entry symbol derived from the AArch64 vector table
  and the x86 IDT and assembly entry stubs.

In each of the first three an architectural interrupt enable between the shape
and the site takes the admission away again.

There is no function-name allowlist. The raw primitives and their callers are
derived from syntax, and an empty primitive census fails rather than passing
vacuously.

"Dominates" is what review round 1 rewrote and rounds 2 and 3 narrowed twice
more. The third arm used to be a bare positional search — any textually earlier
`disable_interrupts()` with no later `enable_interrupts()` admitted the site, at
any nesting depth and under any condition. It now requires that each block
containing the disable but not the site is entered unconditionally, and that
neither the disable nor that construct is `#[cfg]`-gated, since a text ratchet
cannot tell which feature or target profile compiles an attributed statement.

"Entered unconditionally" is now exactly two shapes, and everything else is
conditional: an `unsafe { … }` block, and a bare `{ … }` block whose preceding
code byte is `;` or `{` or which opens the function body. Round 3 deleted the
third shape the arm used to admit — a bare block whose preceding code byte is
`}` — because two spellings of the rule that tried to keep it were both
laundered; see "Round 3 (R162)" below. The deletion is conservative: a bare
block written directly after an `if` statement is genuinely unconditional and is
now refused anyway, which costs such a site its local admission and reddens the
ratchet. No site in the censused tree is in that position — the census totals
are byte-for-byte identical across the deletion.

## Limits of this ratchet

Added in round 3 (findings n2, n3, n4). These are properties of the rule, not
gaps awaiting a fix in this slice, and the "Scope of the claim" section above is
bounded by them. The same list is in the ratchet's own doc comment in
`tests/teardown_structure.rs`, so a reader of the test does not have to find
this document.

**n2 — the mask recognizer trusts two identifiers.** A site is admitted as
"inside a mask" when it is lexically inside a call spelled `without_interrupts`
or `arch_without_interrupts` with a closure argument. The ratchet does not
resolve those paths, and does not check what they do. A same-named wrapper that did not in
fact mask would be believed, and a real mask spelled some third way would not be
seen. This is a text ratchet; the identifiers are the contract.

**n3 — the closure-range admission is lexical, not temporal.** The admission is
"the acquisition's byte offset lies between the closure's braces". A closure
created inside a masked region and stored for later execution outside it would
be admitted anyway. No such site exists in the censused surface today: of the 28
acquisition sites, the 22 admitted by a local shape sit either inside an
`arch_without_interrupts`/`without_interrupts` call, whose closure that API runs
synchronously, or inside one of the two non-closure shapes (the `are_enabled`
false arm, and a dominating architectural disable).

**n4 — synchronous exceptions are outside the model.** The AArch64 mask is `msr
daifset, #3` (`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/cpu.rs`), which sets I and F and
leaves D and A alone; synchronous exceptions are not DAIF-maskable. A
data abort, an instruction abort or an SVC can therefore be taken while a thread
is inside one of these "masked" windows, and this ratchet does not model what
happens then. The narrowing that makes that safe today is not the mask, and
it is worth recording exactly: `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs`
reaches BLOCKING scheduler acquisitions only on EL0-sourced arms — the fault
handlers fork on `from_el0`, taking `terminate_current_scheduler_thread()` (a
blocking `with_scheduler`) on the EL0 side and the `try_lock`-based
`terminate_thread_best_effort` / `switch_to_idle_best_effort` spellings on the
EL1 side, with the in-tree comment "EL1 may have faulted while holding
SCHEDULER, so termination is non-blocking there." A synchronous exception taken
inside a masked EL1 window therefore reaches only `try_lock` acquisitions, where
a failed acquisition degrades a diagnostic rather than deadlocking. That is a
property of `exception.rs`, not of this ratchet, and the ratchet does not enforce it.

**Enforcement.** No gate on this repository invokes this file; see "How the
ratchet is reached, and by what".

**n1 -- entry roots were admitted by bare function NAME.** Confirmed and
repaired. `function_is_entered_with_irqs_masked` compared

The set is now `path::name` identities, built by
`derived_interrupt_entry_identities`, and resolution is by construction rather
than by convention:

- an assembly-branched symbol resolves to the file(s) whose `#[no_mangle]` /

  a static does not capture the next function;
- an x86 IDT handler resolves to `kernel/src/interrupts.rs`, the module whose

  unqualified identifier.
