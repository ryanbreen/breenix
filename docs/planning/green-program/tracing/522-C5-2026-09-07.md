# 522 C5: separating the initialization watchdog from the test-phase budget

## The reading and the decision

Issue 522 item C5 reads, in the survivors plan:

> C5: kernel-entry Phase-1 age consumes later gate budget; aggregate gate ceiling can preempt target windows

Its prescribed next action is to "separate the initialization watchdog from the
gate's own budget", to "begin the test-phase budget at test-phase entry", to
"keep a separately named initialization watchdog for CPU bring-up", and to "give
the exit-kick gate its promised local allowance", while explicitly forbidding
three shortcuts: enlarging the shared 65 s budget, removing the safety ceilings,
and relabelling a live CPU as unresponsive.

Before this change, one constant and one anchor served both purposes.
`kernel/src/test_framework/mod.rs` defined
`PHASE_ONE_LIVENESS_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS: u64 = 65_000`, and
`kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs` published its anchor before initialization with
`kernel::test_framework::begin_phase_one_liveness_budget(timer::rdtsc_serialized())`.
The P20 exit-kick gate in `kernel/src/tracing/providers/teardown.rs` then tested
that inherited anchor first, ahead of its own gate ceiling and its per-wait
ceilings, so every second spent in initialization was a second the gate's own
45-second allowance no longer had.
claim-lint:ok: 522 C5; the pre-change source is fbe821714094, quoted from
`kernel/src/test_framework/mod.rs` and `kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs` at that
commit, and 1 of 1 anchor served both roles there.

## What changed

`kernel/src/test_framework/mod.rs` now defines two separately named budgets with
two separate anchors:

```rust
pub const INITIALIZATION_WATCHDOG_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS: u64 = 40_000;
pub const TEST_PHASE_LIVENESS_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS: u64 = 60_000;
```

with `begin_initialization_watchdog` / `initialization_watchdog_started_at` and
`begin_test_phase_liveness_budget` / `test_phase_liveness_started_at`.

- `kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs` publishes only the initialization anchor, at the
  same point in boot the old anchor was published, and the secondary-CPU
  bring-up wait reads only that anchor.

  **Correction (fix pass below, finding V-1).** The paragraph this replaces
  claimed that publishing the watchdog at 40 s, the same figure as the local
  absolute ceiling, meant "the shared watchdog is not a tighter bound on
  bring-up than the local ceiling already in force" and "a live CPU is given
  up on no sooner than before." That was false: the watchdog is anchored at
  kernel entry, strictly earlier than the bring-up wait's own `start`, so a
  same-magnitude watchdog timed from the earlier anchor reaches its deadline
  strictly before a same-magnitude local ceiling timed from the later one --
  anchoring earlier at equal magnitude is a tighter bound, not an equal one,
  for any nonzero gap between the two anchors, and real pre-wait
  initialization work makes that gap nonzero on every boot. The practical
  effect was that the local ceiling's own diagnostic became unreachable. See
  "Fix pass (review round 1)" below for the correction: the watchdog's ceiling
  is now computed dynamically from the local ceiling itself, and
  `SMP_ONLINE_ABSOLUTE_CEILING_SECONDS`/`SMP_ONLINE_NO_PROGRESS_WINDOW_SECONDS`
  are no longer 40/20 but 15/10.
- `kernel/src/test_framework/executor.rs` publishes the test-phase anchor at the
  top of `run_all_tests`, which is test-phase entry, before any test kthread is
  spawned.
- `kernel/src/tracing/providers/teardown.rs` spends the test-phase budget. The
  gate's parameter is renamed `test_phase_started_at`, and its enclosing-ceiling
  arm compares against `TEST_PHASE_LIVENESS_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS`.

The ceilings and the classification order established by the C4 lane
(`docs/planning/green-program/tracing/522-C4-2026-09-07.md`) are unchanged:
PhaseOneCeiling, then GateCeiling, then AbsoluteCeiling, then NoProgress, then
CounterStall. The 15 s per-wait ceiling, the 45 s gate ceiling, the 8 s
first-progress floor and the 3 s no-progress window are all untouched.
claim-lint:ok: 522 C5; the ordering is pinned by
`tests/teardown_structure.rs::test_phase_budget_is_anchored_at_test_phase_entry`
and read back in 3 of 3 strict boots below.

## Why 60 s, and why that is not "simply enlarging 65 s"

Neither replacement window is larger than the single 65 s clock it replaces:
initialization gets 40 s, the test phase gets 60 s, and they are sequential
rather than shared. The size of the test-phase budget is set by a measurement,
not by taste. The gate is entered part-way into the test phase, so what the gate
actually receives is `budget - (age of the test-phase anchor at gate entry)`.
This round adds a breadcrumb that reports that age directly. Across the three
final strict boots it read 1961 ms, 1802 ms and 2564 ms, so the budget remaining
at gate entry covers the full 45 s ceiling with about 13 s to spare.

## The oracle

`exit_kick_budget_anchor_isolation_test` in
`kernel/src/tracing/providers/teardown.rs` is a `boot_tests`-only aarch64
fixture, registered in `kernel/src/test_framework/registry.rs` as
`exit_kick_budget_anchor_isolation`. It burns a real, bounded 6-second pre-test
interval, then runs one deadline classifier -- ordered exactly as the real
gate's: test-phase budget, gate ceiling, absolute wait ceiling, no-progress --
over three legs. Its budgets are scaled analogues (5 s enclosing budget, 3 s
gate ceiling, 4 s absolute wait ceiling, 2 s first-progress floor, 1 s
no-progress window) so the whole oracle costs about eleven seconds of boot.

Verbatim from `serials/522-c5/strict-boot1-serial.txt`:

```text
[exit_kick_budget_anchor] pre_test_delay_ms=6036 fixture_budget_ms=5000 fixture_gate_ceiling_ms=3000
[exit_kick_budget_anchor] scenario=inherited_kernel_entry_anchor anchor=inherited cause=phase_one_ceiling target=none elapsed_ms=0 budget_age_at_entry_ms=6038
[exit_kick_budget_anchor] scenario=test_phase_entry_anchor anchor=test_phase cause=no_progress target=worker_1 elapsed_ms=2023 budget_age_at_entry_ms=0
[exit_kick_budget_anchor] scenario=gate_window_exhaustion anchor=test_phase cause=gate_ceiling target=none elapsed_ms=3035 budget_age_at_entry_ms=0
```

- **RED leg (the old rule).** `inherited_kernel_entry_anchor` hands the wait the
  pre-burn anchor, which is what the previous code inherited from kernel entry.
  Its age at wait entry is already 6038 ms against a 5000 ms budget, so the wait
  gets `elapsed_ms=0` of window and returns `cause=phase_one_ceiling
  target=none`: the pre-test delay consumed the whole allowance and the verdict
  cannot name anything.
  claim-lint:ok: 522 C5; 3 of 3 boots, `serials/522-c5/strict-boot*-serial.txt`.
- **GREEN leg (this change).** `test_phase_entry_anchor` hands the same frozen
  target the post-burn anchor. The wait receives its full local first-progress
  window and reaches its own actionable verdict: `elapsed_ms=2023`,
  `cause=no_progress`, `target=worker_1`.
- **Real-exhaustion leg.** `gate_window_exhaustion` keeps the target's own
  progress counter advancing, so the no-progress window stays re-armed and the
  wait runs to the gate ceiling: `elapsed_ms=3035`, `cause=gate_ceiling`,
  `target=none`. A genuine whole-gate overrun is still the gate ceiling and is
  still non-CPU-naming.
  claim-lint:ok: 522 C5; 3 of 3 boots, `serials/522-c5/strict-boot*-serial.txt`.

Boot 2 read 0 / 2019 / 3041 ms and boot 3 read 0 / 2028 / 3039 ms on the same
three legs, with the same three causes and the same target attribution: 3 of 3
boots.

## Fix pass (review round 1): closing V-1, V-3, V-6, V-8

Review round 1 of this round returned four findings, 4 of 4 closed here on
branch `tracing/522-c5-phase1-budget`. Two were major (V-1, V-3): the false
"not a tighter bound" claim above, and the two watchdogs' summed worst case
exceeding the strict harness's own hard timeout. Two were minor (V-6, V-8): a
vacuous ratchet assertion, and a breadcrumb missing from a second consumer of
the test-phase budget.

### V-1: the watchdog was anchored earlier than the local ceiling, at equal magnitude, so it fired first

The review's own arithmetic: `INITIALIZATION_WATCHDOG_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS`
(40,000ms) was anchored at kernel entry
(`begin_initialization_watchdog(timer::rdtsc_serialized())`, called before any
initialization work); `SMP_ONLINE_ABSOLUTE_CEILING_SECONDS` (40s) was timed
from the bring-up wait's own `start` (`timer::rdtsc()`), captured strictly
later, after memory management, the generic timer, the RTC and the GIC had
each initialized. Because the watchdog check runs first in the loop, ahead of
the local-ceiling check, and its deadline (kernel entry + 40s) is earlier than
the local ceiling's own deadline (`start` + 40s) whenever that gap is nonzero
-- which real initialization work makes it, on any boot with nonzero pre-wait
initialization time -- the watchdog reached its deadline first. The local ceiling's own
`[smp] Timeout waiting for CPUs: absolute ceiling ...` diagnostic was dead
code in every `boot_tests` boot, and a live-but-slow CPU's promised local
window was silently narrowed by the unmeasured gap. claim-lint:ok: #522 C5
fix pass V-1; reachability restored, see `serials/522-c5-fixpass/strict-boot1-serial.txt:173`
below and the mutation transcript that reddens on reverting this fix.

**Fix.** `kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs` now computes the watchdog's real,
per-boot ceiling after `start` is known, as
`max(published_floor, local_ceiling + measured_gap + margin)`, where
`measured_gap` is `timer::elapsed_ticks(start, initialization_started_at)` --
the actual elapsed time between the two anchors, sampled fresh on the current
boot -- and `margin` is a new `INITIALIZATION_WATCHDOG_LOCAL_CEILING_MARGIN_SECONDS =
3` constant, added so the watchdog's deadline is strictly later than the
local ceiling's own deadline rather than tying with it (a tie would still
leave the local ceiling's diagnostic unreachable, because the watchdog is
checked first). By this construction the watchdog cannot reach its deadline
before the local ceiling does, for any measured gap: both terms
(`local_ceiling`, `margin`) are added to the watchdog side of the comparison
rather than subtracted from the local side.
claim-lint:ok: #522 C5 fix pass V-1; the arithmetic is
`fix_pass_v1_watchdog_not_tighter_than_local_ceiling` in
`tests/teardown_structure.rs`.

The fired-message (`kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs`) was also changed to report
the real computed ceiling (`initialization_watchdog_ceiling_ticks`) rather
than the raw published-floor constant, so the diagnostic stays honest if the
dynamic formula ever extends past the floor.

Measured, `serials/522-c5-fixpass/strict-boot1-serial.txt:173`:

```text
[smp] initialization_watchdog gap_ms=106 local_ceiling_ms=15000 margin_ms=3000 effective_ceiling_ms=20000
```

The real gap (106ms) is small; the published floor (20,000ms) already covers
`local_ceiling (15,000ms) + gap (106ms) + margin (3,000ms) = 18,106ms` with
room to spare, so the `max(...)` resolves to the floor in the normal case
without needing to extend past it -- and would extend automatically if a
future boot's gap grew larger.

### V-3: the two watchdogs' summed worst case exceeded the harness's own hard timeout

`docker/qemu/run-aarch64-boot-test-strict.sh:932` sets
`timeout "${BREENIX_STRICT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS:-90}"`. The pre-fix-pass pair --
`INITIALIZATION_WATCHDOG_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS = 40_000` plus
`TEST_PHASE_LIVENESS_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS = 60_000` -- summed to 100,000ms,
past that 90,000ms hard kill. A boot whose SMP bring-up wait pathologically
consumed its full local window, followed by a test phase that also ran to its
own full ceiling, would be killed by the harness's external `timeout` before
either watchdog's own attributed in-kernel verdict fired -- the same
unattributed `ended_by=hard_timeout` shape
`serials/522-c4/strict-before-sleep-boot1-gate_boot_facts.txt` already
records from an earlier round (`guest_uptime_ms=89357:ended_by=hard_timeout`,
no `cause=` line).

**Fix.** `TEST_PHASE_LIVENESS_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS` could not safely shrink:
the same budget is spent, sequentially after the real gate, by
`exit_kick_worker_window_isolation_test` (about 39s of its own deliberate
per-scenario ceilings: 15,002 + 8,002 + 8,007 + 8,003 + 2ms in
`serials/522-c5/strict-boot1-serial.txt`) and
`exit_kick_budget_anchor_isolation_test` (about 11s), so a healthy boot's real
consumption of the budget is already about 52s of the 60s -- shrinking it
further risks a healthy boot's own self-tests running into the enclosing
ceiling. `EXIT_KICK_GATE_CEILING_MILLISECONDS` (45,000ms) is the round's
central deliverable and was not touched.

That left the initialization watchdog. `SMP_ONLINE_ABSOLUTE_CEILING_SECONDS`
(40s, `boot_tests` only) predates this lane; the comment beside it already
documented that the "realistic starved proof remains far inside this
backstop: <=10s total, with the longest observed individual wait about 8s" --
so the pre-fix-pass 40s ceiling carried 4x headroom over that <=10s total and
5x over that ~8s longest individual wait.
`SMP_ONLINE_NO_PROGRESS_WINDOW_SECONDS` (20s) sits beside it with no separate
realistic-worst-case figure of its own in that comment. Both were halved (40
to 15, 20 to 10), preserving their 2:1 ratio; the fix-pass 15s ceiling still
carries 1.5x headroom over the <=10s total and 1.875x over the ~8s longest
individual wait -- real margin over measured reality, smaller than the
pre-fix-pass 4x/5x but not tight against it.
`INITIALIZATION_WATCHDOG_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS` (the published floor) was
correspondingly reduced from 40,000 to 20,000ms, and V-1's fix (above) is what
makes that reduction safe: the floor is no longer the sole determinant of the
watchdog's ceiling, and the dynamic `max(...)` formula keeps the local
ceiling's own window intact regardless.
claim-lint:ok: #522 C5 fix pass V-3; "realistic starved proof" is a verbatim
quote of the pre-existing `kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs` comment beside
`SMP_ONLINE_ABSOLUTE_CEILING_SECONDS`, not a new claim of this paragraph's
own; 40/10=4, 40/8=5, 15/10=1.5, 15/8=1.875 against that same comment's own

The pair now sums to 20,000 + 60,000 = 80,000ms, ten seconds inside the
90,000ms hard timeout -- the same order of headroom the pre-#522-C5 single
65-second clock left the harness (`90s - 65s = 25s`), just not identical to it
(the finer two-clock split spends that headroom differently: the watchdog's
own margin plus the ten seconds now cited, rather than one shared cushion).

### V-6: the headroom ratchet compared two literals bound in the test, not the real source constants

`test_phase_budget_is_anchored_at_test_phase_entry` asserted
`gate_ceiling_milliseconds + 10_000 <= test_phase_budget_milliseconds` where
both operands were `u64` literals (`45_000u64`, `60_000u64`) written directly
in the test body -- a comparison that cannot redden for any change to the
kernel source it was meant to describe.

**Fix.** A new helper, `parse_u64_const(source, name)`, reads a
`const NAME: u64 = 1_234;`-shaped declaration's real numeric value out of
source text (verified correct by its own unit test,
`parse_u64_const_reads_real_values`). The vacuous assertion was removed from
its original test, and a new dedicated test,
`fix_pass_v6_headroom_ratchet_reads_real_values`, performs the real
comparison against parsed values. `fix_pass_v3_watchdog_and_test_phase_budgets_fit_under_hard_timeout`
(V-3's own test, above) also uses `parse_u64_const` throughout, for the same
reason.

### V-8: the budget's second, deeper consumer had no visibility breadcrumb

`exit_kick_protocol_gate_test` (the real P20 gate) reports its
`anchor_age_at_gate_entry_ms` on entry -- how much of the 60s test-phase
budget was already spent when it started. `exit_kick_worker_window_isolation_test`,
