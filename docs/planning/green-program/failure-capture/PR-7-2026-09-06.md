# Failure-capture PR-7: the soft-lockup report reaches BXCAP

Branch `capture/ftc-pr7-lockup-bxcap`, base `36acca6d50094d794f8ac07a5e675f00bb43edf1`.
Plan: `docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PLAN-2026-09-05.md` section 6,
as re-scoped by the PR-7 implementation plan this round worked from.

## 1. What changed, and what it repairs

`arch_impl/aarch64/timer_interrupt.rs`'s `dump_lockup_state` runs inside CPU0's
hardware timer interrupt. Before this PR it did two things that path may not do.

**It allocated.** The report's first arm called
`task::scheduler::try_dump_state`, which collects a `Vec<ThreadDumpEntry>` and a
`Vec<u64>` while holding the scheduler guard; its second arm called
`process::try_dump_state`, which builds a `Vec` of owned `String`s. Both take the
heap allocator's lock from interrupt context.

**It went quiet in exactly the case it existed to report.** The trace-buffer dump
(`crate::tracing::dump_all_buffers()`) sat INSIDE the scheduler-success arm, so a
refused scheduler lock — the deadlock the detector is looking for — produced one
line, `Scheduler lock: HELD (possible deadlock)`, and no trace history at all.
The report also called `crate::tracing::disable()`, leaving the global trace
switch off for the rest of the boot even though the detector is a returning edge.

The repair is mostly deletion. `dump_lockup_state` is now five raw string writes
— a banner, the stall in seconds and in ticks, a closing banner — around one
`crate::capture::emit(Edge::Lockup, stall_ticks, TARGET_TIMER_HZ)` call. The
bounded BXCAP emitter supplies `EDGE`/`CPU`/`EV`/`CNT`/`RING` before it asks the
scheduler for anything, and reports a refusal as `[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]`
after those sections are already on the wire.

`EDGE.a0` is the stalled CPU0-local tick count. `EDGE.a1` is `TARGET_TIMER_HZ`,
the ticks-per-second `a0` is denominated in; a reader divides one by the other
rather than assuming the tick rate.

## 2. Scope decisions, including the ones that cost something

**The process-manager dump is deleted, not replaced.** `ProcessDumpEntry`,
`ProcessDumpInfo` and `process::try_dump_state` are gone. Leaving that branch
reachable would have left the allocation repair unfinished, and the BXCAP schema
has no process section to move it into. `ProcessManager::all_processes` stays —
it has its own documented consumers. Issue #892 carries the replacement:
a bounded process diagnostic, and bounded per-thread coverage.

**The scheduler snapshot is the existing aggregate one, not a richer new type.**
`BXCAP`'s `THR` rows are per-CPU `cur`/`rq`/`threads`/`blocked`. The old report,
on a SUCCESSFUL acquisition, printed each thread's ELR/x30/SP, an inline-schedule
stack scan, ready-queue membership, PID and name, and per-thread wake/syscall
flags. **That is a real regression in some investigations and no claim of
equivalent diagnostic power is made here.** What the trade buys is the other arm:
a report whose content no longer depends on allocating from an IRQ, and whose
trace section survives a refused scheduler lock. The concrete missing
capabilities are enumerated in #892.

**The trace section is CPU0's newest 32 events plus CPU0 ring accounting**, not
the whole-machine `dump_all_buffers()`. `CNT` is the first 32 nonzero registered
counters in registration order, not the hand-chosen list `dump_trace_counters`
printed. That helper is deleted with the rest.

**The re-entrancy latch is kept as it is.** `IN_CAPTURE` is cleared by the outer
`emit()` returning, so a returning lockup dump permits later episodes and later
panic captures — the two-episode oracle below exercises exactly that. What the
terminal-edge justification does NOT cover, and what this PR states rather than
fixes: if a fault inside a lockup capture abandons the outer frame while that CPU
somehow keeps running, that CPU stays latched and emits no further capture. No
capture fault-containment redesign is claimed.

## 3. The oracle: a real peer-held scheduler mutex

`--features boot_tests,capture_lockup_oracle` (no gate builds it) adds
`kernel/src/capture_lockup_oracle.rs`. On `-M virt,gic-version=3 -cpu cortex-a72
-m 512 -smp 2`:

* CPU0 enters the coordinator after SMP bring-up and before any boot test or
  userspace, with interrupts ENABLED and the boot sequence's existing
  `preempt_disable()` still in force, so its hardware timer keeps reaching
  `check_soft_lockup`.
* A CPU1-pinned kernel thread takes the REAL global scheduler guard through
  `task::scheduler::with_lockup_oracle_hold` — `try_lock_scheduler` inside
  `without_interrupts`, guard alive for the whole closure — and holds it for the
  detector's own `LOCKUP_THRESHOLD_TICKS` plus a 128-tick tail. The threshold is
  read from the timer file's own constant through a cfg-only accessor; it is not
  a second copy of the number.
* The oracle does not call `dump_lockup_state`, `check_soft_lockup` or
  `capture::emit`; it does not write a tick counter, the watchdog baseline or
  `WATCHDOG_REPORTED`; and it does not shorten the threshold.
* It runs twice, releasing between episodes and waiting for real
  context-switch progress, which is what shows `WATCHDOG_REPORTED` recovering and
  `IN_CAPTURE` clearing.

`scripts/run-lockup-capture-oracle.sh` starts QEMU paused, attaches GDB, and
supervises the run through a watchpoint on the coordinator's phase word. GDB sets
NO breakpoint in the detector, the dump or the emitter, so the acceptance serial
comes from an undisturbed run. It samples two things a serial cannot carry, both
at coordinator checkpoints OUTSIDE the held interval:

* the arming precondition — CPU0's `DEFERRED_REQUEUE[0]` must be empty, because
  the exception-return path drains that slot under a BLOCKING scheduler
  acquisition. A nonzero slot is reported as a setup failure rather than cleared.
* the receipts — tick/context-switch/syscall/exit-kick-heartbeat counters at
  acquire and release, which CPU held the guard, and whether the hold ended by
  release or by its hardware-clock fail-safe.

The only guest memory GDB writes is the oracle's own host-ack word.

## 4. Red and green, as measured

### Serial

RED (`docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/serials/pr7-red/main-aarch64-lockup-no-capture.txt`):
origin/main's report with ONLY the oracle scaffolding added. The one nonsemantic
difference is `#[cold]`/`#[inline(never)]` on the original dump, so the optimiser
keeps the symbol the binary guard needs as a root; the body is main's, unchanged.
That run reached the same two episodes — its receipts show
`rcpt_acquired=1`, `rcpt_cpu=1`, `rcpt_held_ticks=5128`,
`rcpt_progress_moved_during_hold=0` on both — printed the soft-lockup banner and
`Scheduler lock: HELD (possible deadlock)`, and emitted **0** `[BXCAP:`
occurrences.

GREEN (`serials/pr7/aarch64-lockup-two-episodes.txt`): two brackets in one boot.

```
[BXCAP:BEGIN v=1 seq=0 edge=LOCKUP cpu=0 ts=... tsfreq=62500000 uptime_ms=5002 arch=aarch64]
[BXCAP:EDGE kind=LOCKUP a0=0x1388 a1=0x3e8]
...
[BXCAP:RING cpu=0 writes=315 dropped=0 kept=315 span_us=... enabled=1]
[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=0 edge=LOCKUP verdict=partial records=45 bytes=2986 truncated=0 sections_skipped=0x20]
```

and the second at `seq=1`, `uptime_ms=10199`, `RING ... writes=678 ... enabled=1`,
`records=47 bytes=3068 truncated=0 sections_skipped=0x20`. `sections_skipped=0x20`
is THR's bit alone: sections 0-4 complete, THR refused, and `verdict=partial`
agreeing with that rather than reading `complete` beside a refusal.

`tests/capture_bxcap_schema_structure.rs` splits the run on its bracket lines and
applies the existing decoder contract to each block, plus five PR-7 legs: the
refusal note, a missing `END`, a bitmap contradicting the rows, a single-episode
serial, and the receipts themselves.

### Binary

`scripts/check-aarch64-lockup-no-alloc.sh` derives the call graph reachable from
`dump_lockup_state` in a LINKED ELF, to a fixed point, resolving direct `bl`, tail
`b`, and indirect `blr`/`br` through `adrp`/`add`/`ldr`/`mov` address
materialisation and the image's own data. It is transitive, unlike the depth-1
`scripts/check-x86-dispatch-no-alloc.sh`, because the shape this PR removed is an
allocation behind an ordinary named helper. Because the walk is transitive the
target set is the true allocation SINKS only.

RED (`serials/pr7-red/binary-guard-red.txt`, and the plain-main
`binary-guard-red-main-plain.txt`), on the unfixed linked kernel:

```
FAIL: 3 allocating call target(s) reachable from the root.
    ...dump_lockup_state -> ...process14try_dump_state -> ...raw_vec12handle_error -> ...handle_alloc_error -> ...___rust_alloc_error_handler
    ...dump_lockup_state -> ...process14try_dump_state -> <alloc::string::String as core::clone::Clone>::clone -> ...___rust_alloc
    ...dump_lockup_state -> ...process14try_dump_state -> ...raw_vec12handle_error -> ...handle_alloc_error
FAIL: 5 analysis problem(s); a missing target is not a clean leaf.
    UNRESOLVED indirect blr x8 in core::fmt::write at ...
```

Two things to read honestly there. The guard reports ONE path per sink, so the
scheduler helper's own allocating path is reachable but not separately printed —
the process helper's path is the one the search reached first. And the unfixed
report also reaches `core::fmt::write`'s vtable dispatch, which the decoder
refuses to resolve; that is a second, independent failure, not the allocation
finding.

GREEN (`serials/pr7/binary-guard-boottests.txt` and `binary-guard-oracle.txt`):
`reachable funcs: 18`, `call edges: 32`, `PASS: 0 allocation sinks reachable`,
0 analysis problems, on both the `boot_tests` and the oracle kernel.

`tests/lockup_capture_guard_structure.rs` builds seven tiny aarch64 ELFs and runs
the real script against each: direct allocation, allocation behind two newly named
helpers, an allocation reached through a `.data` pointer, a tail call, a missing
root, an unresolvable indirect transfer, and a clean non-allocating chain that
must PASS. Those fixtures exercise the analysis; the two files above are the
PR's evidence about real linked kernels.

### Source

`scripts/check-aarch64-lockup-no-alloc.sh --extract-source` prints the dump's item
body plus each local helper in the timer file reached by a syntactically
resolved call, derived from the calls rather than a maintained list. On this
tree it reaches 4 of 4 items: `dump_lockup_state`, `raw_serial_str`,
`print_timer_count_decimal` and `raw_serial_char`.
`scripts/check-critical-path-violations.sh` applies its capture-scoped denylist to
that text, and `--capture-only` gives the strict surfaces a clean exit status —
the full scan is a debt report whose nonzero status is not a pass/fail signal.
Run against the unfixed tree the extractor surfaces both `try_dump_state` calls,
which that denylist denies.

The whole timer file is deliberately NOT added to the strict list: it carries an
unrelated CPU0-regression `panic!` outside the dump's forward call path, and a
guard demanding that panic be moved or line-pinned would be demanding the wrong
change.

* `tests/serial_line_atomicity_structure.rs`: `dump_lockup_state` 60 -> 5, and the
  `dump_trace_counters` row (10) is GONE rather than present at 0 — a 65-site
  reduction in the raw-output census. `print_hex_u64 = 1`, `print_timer_count_decimal = 1`,
  `raw_serial_str = 1`, `timer_interrupt_handler = 6` unchanged.
* `tests/critical_path_logging_census_structure.rs`: **no expected-count change.**
  Neither `dump_lockup_state` nor `dump_trace_counters` ever had an anchor — their
  raw primitives match neither pattern set. A sixth mutation leg now shows that a
  logging macro newly placed inside the dump is rejected as an unexpected `+` row
  despite that absence. No positive allowance was added for the site.
* `tests/capture_path_lock_free_structure.rs`: the strict scope is mirrored from
  the shell extractor and denylist-checked, with four mutation legs. It also
  closes a pre-existing parity gap the widening exposed — the shell denied
  `to_string` and `expect(` and this suite's `DENIED` array carried neither, and
  the parity loop checked only two spellings. It now checks the complete list.
* `tests/teardown_structure.rs`: the scheduler-acquisition ratchet admits
  `with_lockup_oracle_hold` by its local-mask SHAPE, with no exemption and no name
  in the test. Its doc comment's running totals were stale before this round
  (it said 28/27/22/6; the tree printed 30/29/24/6 at base) and now read
  31/30/25/6, which is what the suite prints.
* `scripts/run-lockup-capture-oracle.sh` takes the host-wide QEMU lock. That was
  a finding from `tests/qemu_host_lock_structure.rs` on the first draft, and it
  matters here more than usual: the oracle measures five seconds of GUEST time
  twice, so a starved guest clock would change what it measures.

## 6. What is NOT claimed

* This is a useful single-occurrence artifact, not general deadlock attribution.
  Whole-machine context-switch or syscall progress, or the boot-test heartbeat,
  still prevents a report while another CPU makes progress. A CPU0 hard wedge
  with no timer delivery cannot execute this capture. No host or QMP detector is
  introduced.
* `[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]` does not identify an owner. Both a busy mutex
  and an uninitialised scheduler make `try_liveness_snapshot` refuse, and the two
  share one wire spelling. The record carries 0 owner CPU/TID fields and 0
  wait-for edges. The oracle establishes the hold independently; a production
  reader must not infer the owner from the note.
  claim-lint:ok: the 2 refusal causes and the 1 spelling are at
  kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs's `try_liveness_snapshot` and
  kernel/src/capture/sections.rs's `threads`.
* Other allocating exception diagnostics remain. `task::scheduler::try_dump_state`
  survives with one caller, an aarch64 exception diagnostic at
  `arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs`. It is outside the dump's binary root and is
  disclosed rather than described as gone; issue #893 carries it.
* No cycle count is claimed for the repaired dump. The emitter's normal bound is
  8192 section bytes plus framing; the acquired-lock arm's `try_liveness_snapshot`
  still counts blocked threads by walking `sched.threads`, so its read time
  depends on the thread population. Fixed-size result arrays do not make that walk
  constant-time.
* The two-episode oracle exercises normal return through the emitter, not fault
  recovery inside it.
* `every_committed_fixture_decodes` in the schema suite still censuses the PR-3
  and PR-4 directories only; the PR-7 fixture is a two-capture serial and is
  decoded by its own dedicated test after splitting, not by that census.
