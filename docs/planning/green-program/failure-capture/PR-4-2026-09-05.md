## 1. The gap this round closes

PR-3 built the `BXCAP v1` emitter and wired one edge to it: a self-test behind
`capture_selftest`, a feature no gate builds. 0 of the 3 edges a failing boot
actually takes called it.

What the terminal edges emitted before this round, verified by reading them:

| edge | what it emitted | what a reader got |
|---|---|---|
| x86_64 `#[panic_handler]` (`main.rs`) | `serial_println!("KERNEL PANIC: {}")`, `log::error!` of the same, then `exit_qemu(Failed)` under `testing` | a sentence. `exit_qemu` ends the QEMU process at that line, so anything not already on the wire is gone |
| aarch64 `#[panic_handler]` (`main_aarch64.rs`) | a 5-line banner, then `trace_dump_counters()` | counters, and no ring tail, per-CPU scheduler state or dispatch trace |
| aarch64 fatal postmortem, section 7 (`exception.rs`) | `crate::tracing::dump_all_buffers()` | the raw per-CPU rings -- wide, undecoded, unbounded, and unbracketed |

The x86 side matters more than one handler suggests. 6 of this kernel's 7
terminal x86 exceptions reach `panic!` -- double fault
(`kernel/src/interrupts.rs:495`), kernel page fault (`:1498`), stack-segment
(`:1560`), GPF (`:1790`), divide-by-0 (`:659`) and stack overflow (`:1241`) --

`invalid_opcode_handler` (`:663`), `log::error!`s and spins on `halt()`; this
round does NOT cover it and does not pretend to (section 8).

## 2. What changed

### 2.1 The three call sites

`kernel/src/main.rs`, x86_64 `#[panic_handler]`: after the banner, before
`exit_qemu`.

`kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs`, aarch64 `#[panic_handler]`: after the banner,
before the `wfi` loop. `trace_dump_counters()` stays beneath it -- `[BXCAP:CNT]`
emits at most 32 nonzero counters where that dumps the whole registry (196 of
196 on this profile, from the committed baseline serial).

`kernel/src/lib.rs`, the `#[cfg(test)]` handler. `kernel/Cargo.toml`'s `[lib]`
sets `test = false`, so no profile in this tree compiles it. It carries the
call anyway, so the census has no exemption to carry, and section 8 lists it
among the things not claimed to have run.

Both live handlers pass the panic site's line and column as the two opaque
`[BXCAP:EDGE a0= a1=]` words. On both architectures the committed fixtures read
`a0=0x4d a1=0x5` -- `kernel/src/capture_oracle.rs:77:5`, the same source
location, which is the cheapest available cross-check that the field means
what it says.

### 2.2 Section 7 of the aarch64 fatal postmortem

`dump_fatal_postmortem_section(cpu_id, 7, ...)` now emits the capture as its
first act and keeps `dump_all_buffers()` after it. Sections 0 through 6 are
untouched. The two words are `ESR_EL1` and `FAR_EL1`, read in place with `mrs`
-- this runs inside the exception handler, so both still describe the fault,
and two system-register reads add no lock, no allocation and no serial byte.

The file's constraint is respected: the change adds no `raw_uart_*` call (the
count in `dump_fatal_postmortem_once` stays at the 18 the
unlocked-multi-byte-write census anchors), no logging macro, no lock, no
allocation.

**Departure from the plan, deliberate.** The plan says section 7's
`dump_all_buffers()` "becomes" the capture. It does not, and should not.
`[BXCAP:EV]` emits at most 32 events from the CAPTURING CPU's ring
(`BXCAP_MAX_EVENTS` in `kernel/src/capture/sections.rs`); `dump_all_buffers()`
emits up to `TRACE_BUFFER_SIZE` = 1024 from each of `MAX_CPUS` = 8. Replacing
the second with the first would narrow the evidence on the one path in this
tree that already has the wide version, which is the opposite of what this
program is for. The bounded, self-bracketing record goes FIRST so that a
host-side kill part-way through the dump still leaves a complete capture --
which is the property PR-5's drain will be measured against.
`tests/terminal_edge_capture_structure.rs` pins both halves, and the fixture
shows the order on the wire.

### 2.3 The re-entrancy latch: PR-4's decision, recorded

PR-3's module comment left an explicit question for this PR: the per-CPU
`IN_CAPTURE` latch is one-way, so a fault that carries a CPU away mid-capture
leaves that CPU silent for the rest of the boot.

PR-4 KEEPS it, and changes no byte of `kernel/src/capture/mod.rs`'s logic. A
terminal edge is already the end of the boot, so the second capture the latch
costs is a capture of a machine that was not going to run again; the recursion
a self-clearing latch would allow is a fault loop inside the emitter that
destroys the FIRST capture's bytes too. A depth counter buys the second
capture only where the inner fault RETURNS, and the outer frame already
handles that by clearing the latch itself. The decision and its reasoning are
recorded in that file's own comment so the next reader does not have to find
this document.

### 2.4 `dump_on_panic()` retired

The plan's section 6 assigns it to this PR. A grep over `kernel/`, `docker/`,
`scripts/`, `tests/`, `tools/` and `docs/` returned 3 references and 0 of the 3
was a call: the definition, the re-export in `kernel/src/tracing/mod.rs`, and a
row in the unlocked-multi-byte-write census. 3 of the 3 are gone; the panic
handlers carry a real capture in its place.

### 2.5 The oracle harness

`capture_panic_oracle` rides `boot_tests`, is not implied by it, and is built
by 0 of the 36 gate scripts under `docker/qemu/`.
`kernel/src/capture_oracle.rs` latches once and panics at guest uptime 3000 ms
from `trace_timer_tick` -- the checkpoint `kernel/src/capture/selftest.rs`
measured, and the same call site, so the panic lands in hard-IRQ context where
the aarch64 EL1-fault family and the x86 double-fault/GPF panics originate.

It is a SIBLING of `kernel/src/capture/` rather than a member: that directory's
denylist forbids `panic!`, because a panic from inside a capture re-enters the
path the capture is reporting from. The rule is respected rather than routed
around, and a test asserts the module stayed outside.

### 3.4 What "green" is defined as, and what it is not

`assert_terminal_edge_capture` in `tests/capture_bxcap_schema_structure.rs`
requires: a bracketed `BEGIN`/`END` with matching `seq` and `edge`,
`truncated=0`, a non-empty `EV` tail, exactly 1 `CPU` row and an `EDGE` record,
plus the whole PR-3 capture contract (`records=` against what decodes,
`verdict=` against its own accounting, the `THR` honesty rule).

It deliberately does NOT require `verdict=complete`. The `THR` section asks the
scheduler for a non-blocking read, and a terminal edge taken in hard-IRQ
context loses that race often: **1 of 4** aarch64 panic boots this round came
back `complete`, the other 3 reported the refusal. Demanding `verdict=complete`
would be demanding the race go one way; a capture that states what it could not
read is a working capture. The x86 gate is `-smp 1`, where there is no peer CPU
to lose to, and its 1 of 1 boot was `complete`.

The plan's PR-4 entry says green requires "a `CPU` record per online CPU". It
cannot and should not: `[BXCAP:CPU]` is the capturing CPU's own per-CPU block,
reachable only through its own base register, and a row for a peer would be a
guess. The scheduler-side view of the other CPUs is what the 8 `THR` rows
carry. The suite asserts exactly 1 `CPU` row for that reason.

## 5. Ratchets

`tests/terminal_edge_capture_structure.rs`, 11 tests. Census-anchored on the
`.rs` files under `kernel/src` as they exist on disk -- not a literal list. It
extracts each `#[panic_handler]` body by brace matching over a code mask, and
refuses a body over 4096 bytes as evidence the match ran past the function.

What it pins:

* 2 stub and 3 live panic handlers, both counts anchored. A live handler gutted
  to `loop {}` moves between them.
* Each live handler carries `capture::emit(` and `Edge::Panic`.
* The capture precedes each of the 4 terminating constructs where present in a
  body (`exit_qemu`, `"wfi"`, `hlt()`, `test_panic_handler(`) and follows the
  banner where there is one.
* The postmortem's 8 claimed sections are indexed 0..7 with no duplicate;
  section 7 alone carries a capture; it emits the capture BEFORE the
  `dump_all_buffers()` it keeps.
* `capture_panic_oracle` rides `boot_tests`, is not implied by it, is forwarded
  from the workspace root, guards its own trigger line, and is built by 0 of
  the 36 gate scripts.
* The oracle module is outside `kernel/src/capture/`, and that directory's
  denylist still forbids `panic!` -- so the reason it lives outside cannot
  quietly evaporate.
* `[lib] test = false` is still what makes the library handler uncompiled.

**Correction to a pushed commit message.** `46d3813c`'s message says the
oracle feature "is built by 0 of the 34 gate scripts under docker/qemu/".

## 8. What is NOT claimed

1. **`invalid_opcode_handler` is not covered.** It `log::error!`s and spins on
   `halt()` (`kernel/src/interrupts.rs:663-684`); it does not panic, so the
   panic handler does not see it. 6 of the 7 terminal x86 exceptions are
   covered, not 7 of 7. The plan's section 8 lists this as a follow-up and it
   stays there.
2. **An aarch64 EL1 fault that stops at `[FATAL_REGS]` gets no capture.**
   `dump_el1_fatal_frame_and_dispatch_trace` has 4 call sites and
   `dump_fatal_postmortem_once` has 4; they are not the same 4 paths. The
   PC-alignment-abort face that `EL1_FATAL_FRAME_DUMPS`'s own comment describes
   reaches the register dump without reaching the postmortem, so it reaches no
   capture either. This round did not widen that.
3. **The library panic handler's capture call has not executed.**
   `[lib] test = false` means 0 profiles in this tree compile it. A test pins
   that this is still WHY, so the claim cannot go stale silently, but the call
   itself is unproven code.
4. **The capture does not survive a deadlock in the banner above it.** Both
   handlers emit their banner through `serial_println!`, which takes
   `SERIAL1`'s spin mutex. A panic raised from an interrupt that preempted a
   holder of that lock ON THE SAME CPU deadlocks before the capture is reached.
   That hazard predates this round and this round did not remove it: the
   capture is placed AFTER the banner deliberately, so that the message and the
   state read as one block. Placing it first would trade that for surviving a
   case this round has not observed.
5. **`verdict=complete` is not claimed for the panic edge.** 1 of 4 aarch64
   boots reported it; the other 3 refused the scheduler read and said so. See
   section 3.4.
6. **No cycle measurement.** Section 7 is a source-level reading.
7. **No claim about PR-5.** A `BEGIN` with no `END` is still consumed by 0 of
   the gates; the drain that would make that distinction actionable is the
   next PR.
8. **The 9 pre-existing `serial_println!` sites in `exception.rs` are not
   repaired.** Section 5 says what they are, that this round adds 0 more, and
   what a repair would have to deal with.
9. **The strict gate is not claimed to be deterministically 20/20.** 4 runs at
   3 heads produced 18/20, 19/20, 20/20 and the merged-head result in section
   6.1. Every red is attributed by exact signature to a filed issue.
10. **The re-entrancy latch's cost is stated, not eliminated.** A CPU whose
    capture is carried away by a nested fault stays silent for the rest of the
    boot. Section 2.3 argues why that is the side to err on; it is not a claim
    that no capture can be lost.
11. **The aarch64 fault record's `a0`/`a1` belong to the labeled fault on the
    ordinary path, and to a nested fault on the re-entrant one.** Section 7
    reads `esr_el1`/`far_el1` live, and the `label=` line above it is printed
    on the first entry only. Sections 0-6 run ahead of section 7, so if one of
    them faults, the original invocation abandons that section and a nested
    invocation is the one that reaches section 7 -- the two words are then the
    nested fault's while the label is still the original's, and the record
    does not carry which of the two it is. `dump_fatal_postmortem_once` states that at
    the point the two `mrs` reads happen. The gap is disclosed rather than
    repaired: latching the words at first entry would make them agree with the
    label and would discard the nested fault's, which is the pair that says
    why the postmortem stopped. A live specimen of the re-entrant path was not
    produced this round; the reading is a source reading of the section-claim
    protocol at `exception.rs:485-497`. This is a different absence from item
    2, which is about EL1 faults that reach no capture at all.
