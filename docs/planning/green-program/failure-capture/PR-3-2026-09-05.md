## 1. What changed

A new module, `kernel/src/capture/`, holding the `BXCAP v1` wire format and an
emitter that writes it under the constraints a fault path imposes: no lock, no
allocation, no formatting machinery, no page-table walk, no I/O beyond the raw
serial primitive, and a bounded output size.

**`record.rs`** is the writer. It is the single byte sink -- everything else
in the module emits through it -- and the single place the byte budget is
enforced. `put()` writes through `crate::tracing::output::raw_serial_char`,
one `out` on x86_64 and one volatile store on aarch64, and drops the byte once
`BXCAP_BUDGET_BYTES` of section content is spent, latching `truncated`. The
`BEGIN` and `END` lines are written with the budget suspended, because a
capture that spent its budget still has to be able to say so. The emitted size
is therefore bounded by

    len(BEGIN line) + BXCAP_BUDGET_BYTES + 2 + len(END line)

where the `+2` is the CRLF the writer puts back if the budget cut a record
mid-line. Measured on the shrunk-budget leg: `BXCAP_BUDGET_BYTES = 512`, a
100-byte `BEGIN` line, `bytes=616` reported -- the bound reached exactly.

A record the budget cut is neither terminated nor counted. That is what makes
`records=` mean exactly the well-formed `[BXCAP:...]` lines a reader can
parse, rather than approximately, so the count is checkable against the wire.

**`sections.rs`** emits the sections in the plan's order -- cheapest and most
lock-free first. `EDGE` and `CPU` read per-CPU registers and statics. `EV`,
`CNT` and `RING` read the lock-free trace substrate PR-2 gave usable depth.
`THR`, the only section that wants the scheduler lock, is last.

`THR` reads through `crate::task::scheduler::try_liveness_snapshot`, which
acquires with `try_lock`, masks local interrupts for the guard's whole window
(R153), and fills fixed-size arrays -- not `try_dump_state`, which builds two
`alloc` vectors while holding the guard. When the read is refused the capture
emits `[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]`, leaves `THR`'s bit clear in
`sections_skipped=`, and continues to its `END`. That is the inverse of the
`arch_impl/aarch64/timer_interrupt.rs` soft-lockup arm, which prints
`HELD (possible deadlock)` and then skips the trace-buffer dump that needs no
scheduler lock at all -- going quiet in the one case it exists to report.
Repairing that arm is the plan's PR-7 and is not attempted here.

The snapshot is *sampled early and printed late*: the rows stay last on the
wire so a fault inside the bulk `EV`/`CNT` sections cannot cost the cheap
sections, while the lock is asked for before those sections spend milliseconds
emitting. That ordering is not cosmetic -- see section 6.3 for the measured
difference it made.

Before this PR, `try_liveness_snapshot` had 0 callers in the tree and
`--gc-sections` dropped it from the shipped kernel; its own doc comment names
the AArch64 soft-lockup watchdog as its intended future caller. The capture is
its first real one.

**`selftest.rs`** is the deterministic edge, behind `--features
capture_selftest`. One `edge=SELFTEST` capture per boot, fired from
`trace_timer_tick` at 3000 ms of guest uptime through the same one-shot latch
idiom PR-2's `RING_SPAN` self-check uses in that file. That places it in
hard-IRQ context, which is where PR-4's and PR-7's terminal edges will run it:
a self-test in thread context would not exercise the scheduler-lock refusal
arm and would not show whether the emitter can run with interrupts masked.

`capture_selftest` is not implied by `boot_tests` and no gate builds it
(pinned by `tests/capture_path_lock_free_structure.rs`), so a gated or shipped
kernel compiles neither the trigger nor any call to `emit()`.
`capture_selftest_tiny_budget` shrinks the budget below what one capture
needs, as the anti-vacuity leg for the bound.

**Not wired here:** the terminal edges. Panic on both arches, the aarch64
fatal postmortem's section 7, and the soft-lockup dump are PR-4 and PR-7. This
PR lands the contract with its own oracle and its own mutation table (sections
3 and 4); each edge lands separately with its own.

## 2. The wire format as implemented, and where it departs from the plan

```
[BXCAP:BEGIN v=1 seq=0 edge=SELFTEST cpu=3 ts=367424125 tsfreq=62500000 uptime_ms=3000 arch=aarch64]
[BXCAP:EDGE kind=SELFTEST a0=0xbb8 a1=0xbb8]
[BXCAP:CPU cpu=3 preempt=65536 nr=1 hardirq=1 softirq=0 uptime_ms=3000 q=exact]
[BXCAP:EV cpu=3 i=1007 ts=371088687 type=0x205 n=SCHED_RESCHED_IPI_RECV p=0 f=0x0]
[BXCAP:CNT TEARDOWN_ENTRY_EXIT=139]
[BXCAP:RING cpu=3 writes=478191 dropped=477167 kept=1024 span_us=15410 enabled=1]
[BXCAP:THR cpu=1 cur=1209 rq=0 threads=12 blocked=7 q=exact]
[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=0 edge=SELFTEST verdict=complete records=76 bytes=4799 truncated=0 sections_skipped=0x0]
```

Per-section record caps, so no section can starve the ones after it:
`EV` 32 (newest first-in-order), `CNT` 32 (nonzero only, registration order),
`THR` one row per scheduler CPU slot (8), the rest one row each.

The `CNT` section deliberately does not call `snapshot_all_counters()`: that
returns `[CounterSnapshot; 224]` by value, roughly 19 KiB of stack, on a path
that may be a fault handler on an already-deep stack. Iterating the registry
reads one aggregate at a time into a register.

### 2.1 Departures from the plan's section 4, and why

| Plan | As implemented | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `[BXCAP:DISP ...]` per-CPU dispatch rows | not emitted | The dispatch ring is a private `static mut` in `kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/context_switch.rs`, which is on the critical-path list, and it is aarch64-only. PR-4 wires the aarch64 fatal path, where `dump_dispatch_trace` is already called from section 6 of the postmortem; that is where the reader belongs. `DISP` stays in the schema as a token PR-4 adds -- an addition, so no version bump. |
| `BEGIN` carries `smp=<n>` | omitted | aarch64 has `smp::cpus_online()`; x86 has no equivalent, and it brings up no APs. Emitting a constant `1` there would be a plausible-looking value the reader could not tell from a measurement, which is what the schema's `q=` discipline exists to prevent. |
| `BEGIN` carries `build=<hex>` | omitted | There is no build identifier in the tree; producing one needs a build script this PR does not add. |
| `CPU` carries `cur/prev/pend/idle/rq/last_sched_tick/silence_ms/sched_lock` | `CPU` carries `cpu/preempt/nr/hardirq/softirq/uptime_ms/q`; the scheduler-derived fields moved to `THR` | Those fields need the scheduler read. Keeping them on `CPU` would put a lock acquisition in the section the plan defines as the lock-free one. |
| `THR` is one row per thread | one row per scheduler CPU slot | Per-thread rows need either the allocating `try_dump_state` or a new fixed-capacity scheduler snapshot. The second is exactly the plan's PR-7 item; doing it here would move PR-7's change into PR-3. |
| Oracle green requires `verdict=complete truncated=0 sections_skipped=0x0` | green requires the schema contract, of which `sections_skipped=0x0` is one valid outcome | Whether a non-blocking read is granted is a race by construction: across 8 self-test boots on this branch it was granted 3 times and refused 5. A criterion that demanded `sections_skipped=0x0` would be demanding a race outcome. The suite instead checks the honesty contract -- `THR` emitted, **or** its refusal stated as `[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]` **and** its bit set -- which is a stronger check, because it also fails a capture that goes silent. Both outcomes are committed as fixtures. |
| Ratchet includes a binary guard modelled on `check-x86-dispatch-no-alloc.sh` | not shipped | See section 9. A depth-1 measurement was taken against a real `capture_selftest` x86 ELF and is reported in section 7.3, but no guard was added to the tree. |

`sections_skipped=` is defined here as the complement of the sections that
*completed*. A section refused (`THR` with the lock busy), a section the
budget stopped before, and a section a nested fault ate each leave their bit
set. `verdict=complete` iff `sections_skipped == 0 && truncated == 0`, and the
decoder checks that the word agrees with the accounting beside it.

The refused fixture is the one that shows the section order paying off: the 5
lock-free sections are present, and the only one lost is the section that
asked for the lock.

The tiny-budget fixture is the one that shows the bound binding, and that the
bracket survives it:

```
[BXCAP:EV cpu=0 i=996 ts=377892750 type=0xff3a n=DE
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=0 edge=SELFTEST verdict=partial records=7 bytes=616 truncated=1 sections_skipped=0x3c]
```

The cut record has no `]`, so the decoder does not count it, and `records=7`
matches the 7 that do decode. `END` is on its own line because the writer puts
the terminator back. That is the property PR-5's drain will be measured
against.

The 2 review-round fixtures are the same idea aimed at a single record. At 30
bytes the budget ends inside `EDGE`:

```
[BXCAP:EDGE kind=SELFTEST a0=0
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=0 edge=SELFTEST verdict=partial records=1 bytes=134 truncated=1 sections_skipped=0x3f]
```

`EDGE` emits one record and returns, so no later `open()` refusal can report
the cut on its behalf -- `0x3f` carries EDGE's own bit because the section
returns what `close()` told it. At 44 bytes the `]` lands and the CRLF does
not:

```
[BXCAP:EDGE kind=SELFTEST a0=0xbb8 a1=0xbb8]
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=0 edge=SELFTEST verdict=partial records=2 bytes=148 truncated=1 sections_skipped=0x3e]
```

`close_dangling_record()` supplies the terminator with the budget suspended,
so that line decodes and `records=2` counts it, and `0x3e` leaves EDGE's bit
clear. Both numbers were wrong before this round's fixes; section 10 has the
mutation legs that show it on a boot.

The x86 fixture also carries the other thing the decoder had to learn. Its
`BEGIN` line is not at column 0:

```
[SW]<K>[SW]<1>[SW]<K>[SW]<1>[BXCAP:BEGIN v=1 seq=0 edge=SELFTEST cpu=0 ts=52592580426 tsfreq=2400659520 uptime_ms=3000 arch=x86_64]
```

The x86 scheduler emits raw single-character `[SW]<K>` markers to the same
UART with no newline of their own -- `run-x86-prod-profile-boot-test.sh`
already requires its own marker matches to be substring matches for that
reason. A record PRECEDED by another writer's bytes is intact; a record with
bytes spliced INSIDE it is not, and the decoder rejects that (the #847 shape).
Both directions have their own leg.

### 3.2 What the decoder pins

`BEGIN`/`END` bracketing (a `BEGIN` with no `END` is refused, not scored); the
version gate on both bracket lines, with an unknown major version REFUSED
rather than best-effort decoded; `records=` against what decodes; the `THR`
honesty contract; `verdict=` against `sections_skipped=`/`truncated=`; the
byte bound against `BXCAP_BUDGET_BYTES` read out of `record.rs` per cfg arm;
and the CRLF record terminator the schema names, which `.gitattributes` keeps
in the fixtures for that purpose.

## 6. What this round found in its own work

### 6.1 The byte budget had three enforcement points, so its mutation was invisible on a real boot

The first M1 run reported `truncated=1` **with the guard deleted**. `close()`
carried a second budget test (`remaining < 3`, for a record whose body fitted
but whose `]\r\n` did not) and `open()` a third (refuse a new record once the
budget is spent). Deleting `put()`'s guard therefore degraded the bound from
byte-exact to "budget plus one record in flight" rather than removing it, and
the mutation was visible to the source ratchet and invisible on the wire --
the wrong way round for a property whose job is to be readable in a serial.

`close()` now writes the terminator first and takes its verdict from whether
`put()` dropped any of it, so `put()` is the only place the budget is
enforced. `open()`'s record-boundary refusal stays, and is what makes the
normal case stop cleanly instead of cutting a line. Behaviour on the shipped
build is unchanged: `records=7 bytes=616 truncated=1 sections_skipped=0x3c`
before and after. Landed as its own commit.

### 6.2 The suite's budget lookup read both cfg arms as the same number

`budget_bytes()` matched the cfg attribute on the feature name, and the
ordinary arm is spelled `not(feature = "capture_selftest_tiny_budget")` --
which contains that name. Both arms resolved to 8192, so the tiny-budget
fixture's bound check was 16x looser than it claimed, and the first corrected
M1 run passed a capture 26 bytes over its own limit. It now matches the whole
attribute, and `the_two_budget_arms_are_read_separately` asserts the 2 arms
read as different numbers. Found by running the mutation on a real boot rather
than only in memory.

### 6.3 Sampling the scheduler read late loses it more often

The first implementation asked for the scheduler snapshot at the `THR`
section, after roughly 4.3 KB had gone out over the UART from a masked
interrupt. It was refused on 3 of the first 3 boots. Moving the acquisition
before the bulk sections, while leaving the rows last on the wire, changed the
outcome to 6 granted of 13. Both numbers are small samples and neither is a
rate; what they support is the direction, and the direction is why the code is
written the way it is.

### 6.7 The self-test checkpoint, a wrong finding, and the gate asymmetry behind it

This is the round's one wrong conclusion, corrected in the same round, and it
is recorded here rather than smoothed over because the correction is the
interesting part.

**The checkpoint itself.** 1100 ms was tried first. The aarch64 strict gate is
`-smp 4` and its boot-test output storm is still running then: of 4 boots at
that checkpoint, 1 had another CPU's `[TEST:...]` line spliced into the middle
of the `END` record --

```
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=0 ... sections_skipped[=0xTEST:p0rocess:fo]rk_exit_defer_reclaim_pairing_test:PASS]
```

-- and a second lost PR-2's `[RING_SPAN:` line the same way, failing that
boot's gate score. The signature is #847, the UART interleaving PR-2's own
round doc section 9.3 found and filed; the same shape turned up again in this
round on an unrelated boot at `[STAGE:early:COMPLETE:90/11[1]RING_SPAN:`. A
~4.5 KB capture written a byte at a time by a lock-free writer is a large
target for it. So the checkpoint moved to 3000 ms, where 15 of 15 boots across
this round produced an uncorrupted `END` line.

**The wrong finding.** A `boot_tests,testing,external_test_bins,capture_selftest`
build on beast then produced 0 `[BXCAP:` bytes across both of
`run-x86-boot-tests.sh`'s serials, while the same build's
`[RING_SPAN:cpu=0:...]` line -- whose checkpoint is 1000 ms -- was present.
That was written up as "the x86 profile does not reach 3000 ms", and the
constant was split per architecture on the strength of it. **That conclusion
was wrong**, and the commit that acted on it is corrected by a later commit
rather than rewritten.

**What was actually happening.** `docker/qemu/run-x86-boot-tests.sh` REBUILDS
the kernel with a hard-coded `--features boot_tests,testing,external_test_bins`
before it boots, discarding whatever was built beforehand;
`docker/qemu/run-aarch64-boot-test-strict.sh` validates the kernel it is
handed and issues 0 build commands of its own. So the x86 gate booted a kernel
with the feature compiled out. `llvm-nm` on the ELF the gate actually booted
carries 0 symbols matching `selftest`, which is what settled it -- the earlier
depth-1 no-alloc measurement had found 19 `kernel::capture` symbols, but in
the ELF from the *manual* build, not the one the gate replaced it with.

**Re-measured**, with the gate's feature list patched for the run and the
patch reverted afterwards (`git status` clean): the x86 profile reaches
3000 ms and produces a complete capture at it. So the constant is a single
3000 ms again.

This gate asymmetry is worth carrying forward: **an x86 kernel feature that no
gate builds cannot be exercised through `run-x86-boot-tests.sh` unmodified**,
where on aarch64 the strict gate will boot whatever kernel is present. PR-5,
which measures its drain against this self-test, needs to know that.

**Not claimed:** that 3000 ms is immune to #847, or that 15 of 15 is a rate.
The interleaving is a property of the tree's serial writers, not of this
capture, and this PR does not repair it. What the measurement supports is
which checkpoint to use.

## 8. Hot-path cost, at source level

In a gated or shipped profile the cost is **0, by compilation**: the trigger
in `trace_timer_tick` is inside `#[cfg(feature = "capture_selftest")]` and
`emit()` has no other caller, so `--gc-sections` drops the module.
`tests/capture_path_lock_free_structure.rs` pins that 0 of the 34 gate scripts
under `docker/qemu/` build the feature, censused off disk rather than listed.

Under `capture_selftest`, per timer tick and before the latch fires: one
relaxed `AtomicBool` load, one `saturating_mul`, one compare. After it fires:
the relaxed load and an early return. The capture itself runs once per boot.

`emit()` is marked `#[cold]` and not inlined, so its frame and constants stay
out of the callers that merely reference it.

This is a claim about instruction shape, not a measurement. No cycle count was
taken.

## 9. What is NOT claimed

1. **No kernel issue is closed by this PR.** No failure edge is wired, so no
   currently-open red produces a capture yet. That is deliberate: the contract
   lands non-reversing with its own oracle and mutation table, and each edge
   lands separately with its own.
2. **No binary no-alloc guard is shipped.** The plan's PR-3 text asks for one
   modelled on `scripts/check-x86-dispatch-no-alloc.sh`. What ships here is
   the source denylist plus the shell guard, and section 5 states what a
   source denylist is worth: it sees the spellings it lists, in the files it
   reads. A depth-1 measurement was taken by hand (section 7.3) against a real
   ELF; a measurement is not a guard, and no committed script re-runs it.
3. **No claim that the emitter is safe under memory corruption.** If the ring,
   the counters or the per-CPU statics are themselves corrupted, the capture
   reports corrupted values faithfully. `RING`'s `dropped=`/`span_us=` and
   `sections_skipped=` make that visible; they do not make it safe.
4. **No claim about a rate.** The scheduler read was granted on 3 of 8
   self-test boots. That is a sample, not a probability, and nothing in this
   PR depends on which way it goes -- both outcomes are contract-valid and
   both are committed as fixtures.
5. **No claim that the byte bound is enforced anywhere but in `put()`.** That
   is now the single enforcement point by construction (section 6.1), which is
   what makes M1 observable; it also means a future edit that adds a second
   one would make M1 stop reddening on a boot.
6. **No claim that `records=` detects byte damage within a line.** v1 carries
   no per-line CRC, by the plan's own decision. `BEGIN`/`END` bracketing,
   monotonic `seq`, and `records=` detect truncation and interleaving at
   record granularity.
7. **No claim that the aarch64 fatal path is improved.** Section 7 of the
   postmortem still calls the unbounded `dump_all_buffers()`. Replacing it is
   PR-4.
8. **No claim that the soft-lockup defects are repaired.** `dump_lockup_state`
   still allocates in interrupt context and still goes quiet when the
   scheduler lock is held. `THR` is written as the inverse of that arm; it does
   not fix it. PR-7 does.
9. **No claim that `check-critical-path-violations.sh` passes at this head.**
   It exits 1, on pre-existing `scheduler.rs` sites this branch does not
   touch, and the output is byte-identical to its pre-change output modulo one
   added line.
10. **No claim that #855 or #858 is addressed.** Both are filed, with the
    reading that prompted each, so PR-4 does not rediscover them.
11. **No claim that the aarch64 fixtures and the x86 fixture were produced by
    identical machinery.** The 3 aarch64 fixtures came from the strict gate
    unmodified; the x86 one needed that gate's own feature list patched for
    the run, because it rebuilds the kernel (section 6.7). The patch was
    reverted and `git status` came back clean, and that is recorded in the
    fixture's provenance header rather than left to this document.
12. **No claim about the `[SW]<K>` prefix on the x86 fixture's `BEGIN` line.**
    The decoder accepts a leading foreign prefix and rejects a mid-record
    splice. That is a decoding rule, not a statement that the tree's serial
    writers are safe to interleave; #847 is open and this PR does not touch
    it.
13. **The claim that "the x86 boot_tests profile does not reach 3000 ms",
    made by a commit on this branch, is FALSE and is corrected by a later
    commit on the same branch.** Section 6.7 carries the correction and the
    measurement that produced it. It is left in the history rather than
    rewritten out of it.
14. **No claim that the per-CPU re-entrancy latch is reset after a fault that
    does not return.** `IN_CAPTURE` is cleared by the outer `emit()`
    returning, which is its one reset site, so a CPU whose capture is carried
    away by a nested fault emits no further capture for the rest of the
    boot. That is
    the side this PR errs on, and the module comment now says so; the PR that
    wires real terminal edges is where the trade-off starts to cost
    something. Review finding F5, section 10.
15. **No claim that the aarch64 strict gate is green at this head on this

## 10. The review round

A review of this branch at `254f1396` returned 5 findings: 1 major and 4
minor. 5 of 5 are addressed on this branch, in 6 commits -- one per finding

### 10.1 The findings

**F1 (major) -- a section could report a fragment as complete.**
`Writer::open()` refuses only a record that starts with the byte budget
already spent. A record that starts with a few bytes left runs out MID-write:
its `text()`/`kv_*()` calls drop the rest, `close()` declines to count it, and
the section function returned `true` anyway. `emit()` then set that section's
bit in `completed`, clearing it from `sections_skipped=` -- so the one field a
reader has for learning which section a truncation ate could name a fragment
as a completed section. `EDGE`, `CPU` and `RING` emit one record and return,
so no later `open()` refusal could correct them; the loop sections were
exposed on their last iteration.

`close()` now returns whether the record went onto the wire whole and is
`#[must_use]`, and the 6 section functions return or branch on it at 6 of
their 7 call sites. The remaining site is the `[BXCAP:NOTE sched_lock_held]`
refusal, which discards the verdict deliberately.

Closed with: the `capture_selftest_cut_in_record` budget arm and its fixture
(section 3.1), the fragment-token cross-check in `assert_capture_contract`,
the `writer.close()` census in `tests/capture_path_lock_free_structure.rs`,
and mutation legs M11, M13 and M14.

**F2 (minor) -- `records=` could undercount by one.** The budget ending
between a record's `]` and its CRLF left the record uncounted while
`close_dangling_record()` put the terminator back with the budget suspended,
so the line WAS on the wire and DID decode. `put()` now returns whether the
byte landed and `close()` counts on the `]` itself; `in_record` becomes
`pending_terminator`, so the repair supplies the bytes that did not land
rather than a fixed pair.

Closed with: the `capture_selftest_cut_at_terminator` budget arm and its
fixture, and mutation leg M12.

**F3 (minor) -- two `bytes=` values for one x86 run.** Section 7.2 said
`bytes=4422`; the fixture the same paragraph pointed at said `bytes=4436`.
Repaired by re-measuring at the fix head and re-taking the fixture from that
boot, so both now read `bytes=4424` and come from the same serial. Section
7.2 also now says why two runs of one profile differ at all.

**F4 (minor) -- a literal gate list where a census belonged.**
`the_selftest_edge_is_feature_gated_and_no_gate_builds_it` named 4 script
paths out of the 34 under `docker/qemu/`;

scan now walks the directory. No live gap was found: that gate builds
`--features boot_tests`, and the check reports the same 0 offenders before and
after.

**F5 (minor) -- the one-way re-entrancy latch was undocumented.** Behaviour
unchanged and deliberate; what was missing was the sentence saying so, and
the note that PR-4 and PR-7 inherit the trade-off. Now in the module comment
and in section 9 item 14.
