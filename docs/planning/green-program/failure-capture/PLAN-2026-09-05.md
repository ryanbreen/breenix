## 1. The finding in one paragraph

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
The tree already owns nearly every *ingredient* of a debuggable capture: a
lock-free 16-byte-record ring, 196 registered counters, lock-free serial/UART
writers, a per-CPU dispatch ring, a genuinely good aarch64 fatal postmortem with
per-section re-entrancy claiming, a soft-lockup detector, a strand oracle, and a
source-derived memory-dump decoder. What it does not own is **a capture that
fires on the edges that actually fail, in a format a scorer can read, that
survives the gate's own SIGTERM — and any host-side record of whether the guest
was even given the CPU to reach the marker it was scored against.** Four
verified holes make today's reds undebuggable from one occurrence:

1. **The ring holds about one second of aarch64 history** while the strict
   gate's failure edge sits at ~12.85 s of guest uptime.
2. **The x86 fatal and panic paths dump no trace, no scheduler state and no
   counters at all**, and emit through a `spin::Mutex`.
<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
3. **No gate opens any out-of-band channel to QEMU** — `-s`, `-gdb`, `-qmp` and
   `-monitor` are absent from all five gate command lines.
4. **No gate records a single per-boot host fact**, so a guest that was starved
   of CPU and a guest that was wedged produce a byte-identical verdict.

Hole 4 is both the cheapest to close and the one that gates the interpretation
of everything else: a kernel capture read without knowing the guest ran at 41%
of wall-clock rate is a misattribution machine. It is therefore PR-1.

### 2.2 The ring-depth arithmetic — the single most load-bearing number here

`TARGET_TIMER_HZ = 1000` on aarch64 (`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/timer_interrupt.rs:46`),
`PIT_HZ = 200` on x86 (`kernel/src/time/timer.rs:19`). `trace_timer_tick` records
one ring event per tick per CPU whenever tracing is enabled
(`kernel/src/tracing/providers/irq.rs:131-139`), and is called unconditionally
from both timer handlers — `timer_interrupt.rs:730` and the Tier-1
`kernel/src/interrupts/timer.rs:51`. The ring holds 1024 entries per CPU.

**As arithmetic on those four verified constants — not as a measurement taken in
this pass:**

* aarch64: `1024 / 1000 Hz ≈ 1.02 s` of history per CPU, **as an upper bound**,
  before a single syscall, context-switch or queue-state event is counted.
* x86 (`-smp 1` in both x86 gates — `run-x86-boot-tests.sh:454`,
  `run-x86-prod-profile-boot-test.sh:1053`): `1024 / 200 Hz ≈ 5.1 s`, same caveat.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
Set that against #826's own measurement: `[EXEC_SMOKE:TARGET_OK]` lands at
**~12.85 s of guest uptime** on a healthy strict boot, and the kill lands at
~17.7–18.6 s. A one-second tail of `TIMER_TICK` records nothing about a failure
edge twelve seconds into the boot. This is why the aarch64 fatal postmortem's
section 7 — an unbounded `dump_all_buffers()` at
`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs:592-594` — looks comprehensive and has
not made the open reds debuggable.

### 2.3 The aarch64 fatal postmortem is the good half of the tree

`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/exception.rs`:

* `dump_fatal_postmortem_once(label)` at `:501`, latched once per CPU via
  `FATAL_POSTMORTEM_CAPTURED` (`:33`, `:509`).
* **Eight sections, each individually claimed** through
  `dump_fatal_postmortem_section` (`:485`) against
  `FATAL_POSTMORTEM_SECTIONS_CLAIMED` (`:57`) — so a fault *inside* a section
  costs that section and the rest of the dump still lands. **This is the
  re-entrancy pattern the new capture copies verbatim.**
* `[FATAL_REGS]` register dump at `:224` and `:295`; a bounded UART guard with a
  give-up at `:76` so two faulting CPUs interleave rather than deadlock.
* Section 7 is the unbounded `crate::tracing::dump_all_buffers()` (`:592-594`).

Adjacent state it already reaches: the per-CPU dispatch ring —
`DISPATCH_RING_SIZE = 8`, `MAX_CPUS_TRACE = 8`, fields
`tid/old_tid/elr/spsr/x30/sp/path/from_el0`
(`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/context_switch.rs:3019-3042`, dumped at `:3098`).
**It carries no timestamp field**, so it cannot be merged onto one time axis with
the trace ring. The lock-free UART writers are at `:3147`, `:3157`, `:3166`, `:3183`.

### 2.4 The panic and x86 fatal paths are the bad half

* **aarch64 panic** (`kernel/src/main_aarch64.rs:2045-2063`): `serial_println!`
  banner, then `trace_dump_counters()` only (`:2056`) — no ring tail, no
  scheduler state, no dispatch trace, no postmortem. Then `wfi`.
<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
* **x86 panic** (`kernel/src/main.rs:2470-2486`): `serial_println!` +
  `log::error!`, then `exit_qemu(Failed)` under `testing`. **Zero trace state**,
  and the process ends at that line, so anything not emitted before it is lost.
* `serial_println!` takes `SERIAL1.lock()`, a `spin::Mutex`
  (`kernel/src/serial.rs:15`, `:118-119`). **The x86 fault path emits through a
  lock.**
<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
* **Which x86 terminal exceptions actually panic** (verified this pass, and a
  correction to the Opus doc's framing): double fault `panic!`s at
  `kernel/src/interrupts.rs:495`, kernel page fault at `:1498`, stack-segment at

  **`invalid_opcode_handler` is the sole exception that never panics** — it
  `log::error!`s and spins on `halt()` (`:663-684`). A panic-handler hook
  therefore covers six of the seven x86 terminal edges; only invalid-opcode
  escapes it.

### 2.5 The existing hang detection has two live defects

`kernel/src/arch_impl/aarch64/timer_interrupt.rs`:

* `check_soft_lockup` (`:999`), CPU-0 only, threshold
  `LOCKUP_THRESHOLD_TICKS = TARGET_TIMER_HZ * 5` (`:192`). Its liveness signals
  are **whole-machine** — `context_switch_count()` and `SYSCALL_TOTAL.aggregate()`
  (`:1000-1034`) — so one busy CPU masks three dead ones.
* **Defect 1 — it allocates in interrupt context.** `dump_lockup_state` (`:1061`)
  calls `crate::task::scheduler::try_dump_state()` (`:1074`), which builds
  `alloc::vec::Vec<ThreadDumpEntry>` at `kernel/src/task/scheduler.rs:1147`.
  That is a heap allocation from a hardware timer interrupt — the shape
  `scripts/check-x86-dispatch-no-alloc.sh` exists to forbid.
* **Defect 2 — it goes silent exactly when it matters.** The trace-buffer dump at
  `:1214` sits *inside* the `if let Some(info) = try_dump_state()` arm; the
  `else` arm at `:1215-1217` prints only `HELD (possible deadlock)`. So when the
  scheduler lock is held — the deadlock case — the gate loses the trace dump,
  **which needs no scheduler lock at all.**

Both defects verified by direct read. Under "any failure you find is your
problem" they are owed a repair by this work: PR-7.

## 4. Capture schema — `BXCAP v1`

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
One record per line, `\r\n`-terminated, `TOKEN key=value` throughout, no spaces
inside a value, every numeric decimal or `0x`-hex. The bracketed-marker style
matches what the tree's scorers already grep (`[FATAL_REGS] label=…`,
`[heartbeat] tid=…`, `[TTBR0_ASID_CENSUS:…]`).

```
[BXCAP:BEGIN v=1 seq=<n> edge=<EDGE> cpu=<id> ts=<raw> tsfreq=<hz> uptime_ms=<n> arch=<aarch64|x86_64> smp=<n> build=<hex>]
[BXCAP:EDGE <edge-specific key=value pairs>]
[BXCAP:CPU cpu=<id> cur=<tid> prev=<tid> pend=<tid> idle=<tid> rq=<n> nr=<0|1>
           preempt=<n> hardirq=<n> last_sched_tick=<n> silence_ms=<n> sched_lock=<free|held> q=<exact|racy|derived|unavail>]
[BXCAP:THR tid=<n> pid=<n> st=<R|X|B|S|C|T|D|I> onq=<cpu|-> elr=<hex> x30=<hex> sp=<hex>
           wake_site=<name> block_site=<name> dwell_ms=<n> q=<…>]
[BXCAP:DISP cpu=<id> i=<k> path=<K|U|I|R|F|B> old=<tid> new=<tid> elr=<hex> spsr=<hex> x30=<hex> sp=<hex>]
[BXCAP:EV cpu=<id> i=<k> ts=<raw> type=<0xhhhh> n=<NAME> p=<n> f=<hex>]
[BXCAP:CNT <NAME>=<total> [c0=<n> c1=<n> …]]
[BXCAP:RING cpu=<id> writes=<n> dropped=<n> span_us=<n> kept=<n>]
[BXCAP:NOTE <one line, for a section that refused or faulted>]
[BXCAP:END v=1 seq=<n> edge=<EDGE> verdict=<complete|partial> records=<n> bytes=<n> truncated=<0|1> sections_skipped=<hex>]
```

**Rules that make it scorable and honest:**

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
* `BEGIN`/`END` bracket the capture. **`BEGIN` without `END` is the definition of
  a truncated capture** — the exact signal the gate needs to separate "the kernel
  never captured" from "the kernel captured and we cut it off". That distinction
  is what PR-5's drain is measured against.
<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
* `v=` on both `BEGIN` and `END`. A decoder **refuses** an unknown major version
  rather than mis-decoding. Bumped on any field removal or semantic change, never
  on an addition.

  a nested capture (a fault during a capture) is visible as interleaved `seq`.
* `sections_skipped` is the claim-bitmap complement — it names, mechanically,
  which sections a nested fault ate. Directly modelled on the verified
  `FATAL_POSTMORTEM_SECTIONS_CLAIMED` pattern.
<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
* `truncated=` reports a spent byte budget, so evidence loss is never silent.

* `verdict=complete|partial` and per-row `q=` (`exact|racy|derived|unavail`)
  carry the honesty. A kernel-side row is `exact` only after one successful
  scheduler `try_lock`; it never waits. A section that could not be read says so

* Every record is `grep -o`-able: `[BXCAP:EV ` lines alone reconstruct the ring;
  `[BXCAP:CPU ` lines alone reconstruct the scheduler snapshot.

**Deliberately excluded from v1** (with reasons, both departures from Sol's KCAP):

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
* **No per-line CRC32.** It costs a CRC implementation on a lock-free fault path
  for a benefit that `BEGIN`/`END` bracketing, monotonic `seq`, and the `records=`
  / `bytes=` counts already largely deliver. Revisit in v2 if interleaving proves

* **No promise of a globally total event order.** Per-CPU slot order is

* **Lock ownership is reported only where the tree already tracks it.** A general

## 5. The hang-detector decision

**Decision: host-owned and staged. The gate is the authority for a hang; the
guest is the authority for a fault. Neither doc's primary is adopted whole.**

The two designs genuinely disagreed here, and the disagreement dissolves once
#826's actual data is taken seriously: **#826 is not a hang.** Its heartbeat
cadence held at ~1002–1006 ms right up to the final line, and its guest clock
advanced at 41% and 53% of host wall-clock rate. The guest was progressing when
it was cut off. No in-guest watchdog can or should fire there, and no GDB
snapshot is needed to see it.

**Tier 0 — serial-derived progress measurement (PR-1). This is the detector for
the common class.** The guest already emits `uptime_ms` about once a second.
Differencing guest uptime against host wall clock at boot start and at kill
yields the starved-versus-wedged split *for free*: no new channel, no guest
cooperation, no symbol loading, no new failure mode inside the gate. Combined
with `ended_by=`, QEMU CPU-time and the concurrent-QEMU count, it turns #826's
in-guest inference into a host-side measurement. This is the whole of #827's
"suggested shape" and three of the three facts #826 says it needs.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
**Tier 1 — passive QMP `stop` + `dump-guest-memory`, non-PASS boots only (PR-6,
conditional).** For a guest that really is wedged — all CPUs spinning or with
interrupts masked, the #576/#626/#635 family — the host is the only authority
that still works. A QMP memory dump is *purely passive*: it executes no guest
code, so it cannot deadlock, cannot perturb, and cannot destroy the evidence it
was invoked to collect. It yields the ring bytes verbatim and
`scripts/trace_memory_dump.py` already decodes exactly that layout.
`scripts/forensic-capture.sh` already implements the mechanism; it needs one
`-qmp` flag on the gate and `socat`.

**Rejected for the gate path: GDB `call trace_dump()`.** Both designs reject it
and they are right, for the same reason: `call` executes guest code on the vCPU
whose state is the thing under investigation. On the #635 face the register file
is garbage and SP may not be a valid stack; a `call` there can hang the batch and
destroy the `[FATAL_REGS]` evidence. It remains the correct *interactive* RCA
tool, which is why CLAUDE.md prescribes `gdb_session.sh` for that.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
**Rejected as an authority, and deferred entirely: the in-guest progress
watchdog.** Opus made it the primary; this plan does not adopt it, for four
stated reasons. (i) It does not serve the first-PR requirement at all, because
the most common recent failure class is provably not a hang. (ii) It is the only
item in either program that adds a recurring hot-path hook, and the only one
whose anti-vacuity leg — an ordinary idle boot must produce zero captures — is
genuinely hard to get right. (iii) Its headline exemplar, **#609, is closed**.
(iv) A watchdog that cannot be scheduled, or whose timer is dead, cannot report
the failure it exists to report; the host path has no such blind spot.
It is deferred with an explicit arming condition (§8), not deleted: its real
advantages — it works on Parallels, VMware and real hardware where there is no
QMP, and it captures at the moment of the stall rather than at the ceiling — are
worth having once a red demonstrates the need.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
**Sol's two-snapshot A/B classification is adopted in substance, not in
mechanism.** Measuring *movement* between two host-side samples is the right
idea and it is what separates `progressing_at_ceiling` from `stuck`. This plan
takes those two samples from the serial-derived guest clock and QEMU CPU time
(PR-1) rather than from two GDB all-stop snapshots — the same discrimination at a
fraction of the cost and with no new failure mode in the gate. The richer
register-level A/B remains available on top of PR-6 if PR-1's classification
proves too coarse.

### PR-3 — `[BXCAP]`: schema, bounded lock-free emitter, deterministic self-test edge

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->

## 8. Open questions

**Q1 — `TICK_SAMPLE` and `RING_SPAN_FLOOR_MS` (PR-2).** The strict gate's failure
edge is at ~12.85 s of guest uptime, arguing for ≥ 20 s of span, i.e.
`TICK_SAMPLE ≈ 20` on aarch64 before other event traffic. But full-density
`TIMER_TICK` is genuinely useful for the timing-jitter questions #644 and #536
live in. *Proposal:* one constant; pick it so the **measured** span in PR-2's own
oracle output clears the floor with margin, not from this document's arithmetic;
a jitter investigation re-runs with `TICK_SAMPLE=1` deliberately.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
**Q2 — Should any capture read a page table?** The absolute constraint says no in
a fault path, and #576/#626's stated closing evidence is exactly a VA-0 walk. The
honest options are (i) never, closing those two through GDB/QMP instead; or
(ii) a **deferred** walk — the fault path records only the raw
`TTBR0_EL1`/`TTBR1_EL1` values, which the tree already dumps, and the *decoder*
walks the tables offline out of a QMP core. **(ii) is right**, and PR-6 is its
scaffolding, but the walk itself is out of scope here.

**Q3 — Syscall tid attribution.** Packing the calling tid into the free upper half
of the `SYSCALL_ENTRY` payload would make lane attribution exact. It changes the
payload semantics of a provider function called from a Tier-1 line
(`syscall/handler.rs`), and aarch64 has **no** lock-free current-tid reader — the
x86 twin is `per_cpu.rs:472`, and `per_cpu_aarch64.rs` has no equivalent. Left
out of PR-2 on purpose; the decoder's "last `CTX_SWITCH_ENTRY` on this CPU lane"
fallback stands, with mis-attributions flagged rather than hidden. Worth its own
PR and its own Tier argument.

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
**Q4 — Ring size versus sampling.** Growing `TRACE_BUFFER_SIZE` from 1024 to 4096
costs 16 CPUs × 64 KiB = 1 MiB of `.bss` and buys 4× span with **no** information
loss. Sampling costs nothing and loses tick density. They are not exclusive. This
plan proposes sampling first because it is a four-line change with a clean
mutation; the size bump is a kernel-image-footprint judgement for the operator.

**Q5 — Byte budget.** `BXCAP_BUDGET_BYTES` trades evidence against a capture that
outlives the gate's patience. Low tens of KiB per capture seems right, but it
should be **measured** against the drain window in PR-5's oracle, not guessed.

**Q6 — `DISPATCH_TRACE` has no timestamp** (`context_switch.rs:3024-3033`).
Adding one is 8 bytes × 8 entries × 8 CPUs = 512 bytes plus one `mrs cntvct_el0`
per dispatch, and it is what lets `DISP` and `EV` share one time axis. It is a
change on the dispatch path, so it wants its own PR and its own timing argument.
Not folded into PR-4.

**Q7 — `invalid_opcode_handler`.** The one x86 terminal edge PR-4 does not cover,
because it halts without panicking. A one-line `capture::emit` before its halt
loop closes it. Fold into PR-4 or take as a trailer?

**Q8 — Lock ownership.** The schema reports only locks the tree already tracks.
A general "who holds what" registry would answer a class of deadlock reds in one
occurrence, and #580/DEBT-3 established the blocking-primitive inventory is
exactly ten. **Not proposed here** — kernel-wide instrumentation with its own
hot-path cost deserves its own design, not a rider on this one.

**Q9 — The other two gates.** PR-1 instruments the strict gate only, because it
owns #826/#827/#828. The prod-profile and service-sequence gates want the same
`.facts` sidecar. One follow-up each, same oracle, once PR-1's shape is settled.

## 10. Where the two designs differed, and why this plan chose as it did

<!-- claim-lint:ok: archived plan artifact quoted verbatim; this claim is the plan's own, bounded by its own section 9, and is not re-asserted by this round -- see docs/planning/green-program/failure-capture/PR-3-2026-09-05.md -->
The designs agreed on more than they disagreed: one versioned, greppable,
one-line-per-record schema; a bounded lock-free emitter that never allocates,
locks, formats or walks a page table; two producers (kernel at a terminal edge,
host for a live guest); GDB `call` into the guest rejected for the gate path; and
a decoder that derives its constants from the kernel sources. **Four real
disagreements remained.** *PR order:* Sol opened with the schema and decoder,
reaching host facts only at its sixth PR, so nothing was debuggable until the
program was nearly complete; Opus opened with gate host facts. This plan takes
Opus's ordering, because the brief requires the first PR alone to make the
#826-class red debuggable and because a kernel capture read without knowing the
guest ran at 41% of wall-clock rate is a misattribution machine — host facts are
the precondition for interpreting every capture that follows, not a late
refinement. *Hang detector:* Opus made an in-guest progress watchdog primary,
Sol made a host GDB two-snapshot A/B classification primary and explicitly
refused a watchdog. This plan takes Sol's principle — the host is the authority
for a hang — but not its mechanism, because #826 is provably *not* a hang and its
discrimination is already available for free by differencing the guest's existing
`uptime_ms` heartbeat against host wall clock; the register-level A/B is deferred
into a conditional QMP PR, and the watchdog is deferred entirely with a stated
arming condition, partly because its headline exemplar #609 turns out to be
closed. *Schema:* the plan keeps Opus's `BEGIN`/`END` bracketing, byte budget and
`sections_skipped` bitmap (all modelled on the real, verified
`FATAL_POSTMORTEM_SECTIONS_CLAIMED` pattern) and Sol's version gate, tri-state
verdict and per-row `quality` field, but drops Sol's per-line CRC32 as unearned
cost on a fault path for v1. *Program size:* Opus proposed ten PRs and Sol seven;
this plan lands seven, and the shrink is not arbitrary — verifying that six of
the seven x86 terminal exceptions reach `panic!` collapsed Opus's separate x86
exception-preamble PR into the panic PR, and deferring the watchdog removed the
largest and riskiest item in either program. Finally, verification against
current `main` cost both docs something: Opus's `BREENIX_GATE_TMP` ride-along had
already landed, its "serials end mid-line" was true of one of two boots rather
than both, both docs' line numbers had drifted, and neither noticed that #609 is
closed or that #694 is a test-logic defect no capture can fix.
